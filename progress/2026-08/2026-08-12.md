### C42 was running and still not firing — the bug was mine, in the wiring

defect. A test that cannot fail on the bug it was written for is decoration.

### C43 second half — the race, and why my first fix was not one

`coordinator-agent` measured this while performing the C42 bootstrap restart I had asked for, which
is worth noting on its own: doing the thing I requested is what exposed the next defect. They killed
supervisor 489217, verified it dead with `ps`, relaunched immediately — and the new process lost the
race against the **dying** holder's flock release, logged the contention, exited 0, and died. For
~90 seconds nothing would have relaunched the daemon if it had died. That is precisely the condition
that went unnoticed for ten days from 2026-07-29.

**My first C43 fix would not have helped.** I had made contention *report* who holds the lock and
whether it is alive. In this race the holder is still alive while releasing, so it would have
printed `(ALIVE)` and exited 0 — accurate, unhelpful, and the gap still open. Evidence about a race
is not a fix for the race, and I would rather record that than let the first fix look sufficient.

Fixed with a bounded `flock -w` (15s). A dying holder releases in milliseconds so the relaunch wins;
a genuinely running one holds for its life so we still report and exit 0. I chose the retry over
exit-non-zero deliberately: for `once` a skip is the *normal* case, and paging on every ordinary
tick is how a real alarm gets ignored.

The test carries a **contrast** rather than just an assertion — it proves the old `flock -n` form
*loses* the same race under the same fixture. Without that, a passing test could just be describing
a machine fast enough not to race, and I would not know which.

### The auditor's retraction corrected my correction

I adjudicated the `--validate-only` dispute from message timestamps: fix committed 00:55:34, auditor's
reply 00:55:35, therefore they read a tree the fix had already repaired. The auditor re-adjudicated
and found something better: **their refutation posted at 00:55:14 — twenty seconds *before* the
commit.** So they never read the repaired commit at all. What they read was `mainB`'s **uncommitted
in-flight working tree**.

That is a different and worse mechanism than the one I described, and it is the one that generalises.
A committed fix at least leaves a timestamp an adjudicator can compare against; an uncommitted edit
leaves nothing. The working tree shows repaired code with no marker that it is newer than the claim.

It is also exactly the hazard that bit me this morning, when I cited `969244d8` for content that was
`mainB`'s uncommitted hunk. Two of us, twelve hours apart, misled by a shared clone presenting
someone else's in-flight work as the record.

So the standing lens needs correcting, and mine was the weaker version. Not *"pin the commit the
reporter saw"* — necessary but insufficient, since the tree in front of you may contain edits that are
in no commit at all. The test is: **when adjudicating a peer's finding in a shared clone, read from a
ref, never from the working tree.** `git show <commit-at-report-time>:<path>`. The working tree is the
one surface that can show you a repair with no evidence that it is a repair.

Worth recording who fixed what: the auditor caught their own error, re-derived the timeline against
git, and retracted unqualified inside twenty minutes. The half of the mechanism I got wrong is theirs.
