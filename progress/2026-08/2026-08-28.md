## Round 5 (2026-08-30) — chain fusion: three attempts, all negative; conclusion recorded

Attempted the elementwise chain fusion three ways to cut the ~46ms of non-gemv dispatch:
1. Generic per-op fused kernel (reverted: pp -20%, scalar loop).
2. SIMD vec-function slices (neutral: 13.95 vs 14.06, -3% pp).
3. Pattern-specialized kernels (SCALE+UNARY+SCALE, ADD_N, ADD+UNARY+MUL...) with the compiler-vectorized per-element loops (reverted: -3% decode, neutral pp).

Bugs found along the way (all fixed or reverted):
- Strided VIEW srcs read as flat-contiguous (contiguity guard).
- The fused kernel must write the chain's INTERMEDIATE dsts (other consumers read them; the llama-arch roundtrip caught it: first divergence at logit 256, ~1e-3).
- The vec silu/sigmoid use SIMD exp2 approximations -> bit-parity with libm-expf is impossible (dropped the unary patterns).
- The llama arch's model-saver roundtrip still failed with the ADD-only fusion; root cause not found (suspected: node-order/fusion-selection interplay across the save/load), which alongside the negative A/B settled the revert.

CONCLUSION (3 independent measurements): the per-node cost (~5-8us) is compute+dispatch, not barriers; the fused kernels' own overhead eats the barrier savings. The ~46ms is the elementwise compute at batch-1, which only a fundamentally different execution model (fewer, fatter ops via kernel fusion of the GEMM+activation pairs) would cut. The mrope/decay graph chains would save ~1-2% - not worth the shared-code risk. The CPU decode (~12.5-14.9 t/s at the interleave baseline) is at the practical ceiling of the current kernels; the 20-60 t/s target needs the gemv/activation kernel work (a multi-day AK-CPU-program-scale effort).

## Round 6 (2026-08-30) — gemv software pipelining: clean-room win, in-situ loss, reverted

**The experiment**: software-pipelined batch-1 gemv (4-row accumulators + prefetch-8) in both one_chunk kernels. Clean-room microbench: IQ3_S 114.6 -> 177.9 GB/s (+55%), Q8_0 79.5 -> 94.2 (+18%).
