## B11 closed as a NON-QUESTION: there is no missing 38%, my premise was wrong

I claimed plain decode moves 4.15 GB/token against ~3 GB of active 4-bit weights, leaving ~38%
unexplained. Both of my inputs were wrong:

- **6.671 B active params, not 6.0** (+11.2%)
- **4.995 effective bpw, not 4.0** (+24.9%) — **`IQ4_XS-uniform` is not uniform**: lm_head q6_K,
  `attn_qkv` q5_K, `ffn_down_exps`/`hc_*_up` iq4_nl, router `ffn_gate_inp` **F32**

**1.112 × 1.249 = 1.389.** The missing 38%, exactly and entirely. Ground truth from a from-scratch GGUF
reader is **4.1656 GB/token**, agreeing with the live-graph ledger to **+0.24%**.

The measurement was never in doubt — 51.3 GB/s was derived as `12.349 × 4.156`, and the 4.156 came from
instrumenting the live decode graph (`GGML_CPU_PROF`), re-derived independently at 4.1558 GB. Not file-size
division, which would have given 98.4 GB. **The number was right; the interpretation was mine.** Worth
noting the shape of the error: I compared a measured quantity against a remembered spec sheet and treated
the gap as a property of the system rather than of my memory.

### Three findings worth more than the question was

1. **The GatedDeltaNet stack is 30.3% of the token (1.261 GB) — as large as all expert traffic combined**,
   and `attn_qkv` alone (648.8 MB) exceeds lm_head. "6B active, mostly experts" is the wrong mental model;
   experts are under a third of the bytes. This should reshape where Axis B looks.
2. **The F32 router costs 251.7 MB/token — 6.0% of the byte budget for 0.25% of the active parameters.**
3. The ledger is weights-only, omitting ~226 MB/token of GDN recurrent state (113 MB `GET_ROWS` + 113 MB
   `CPY`) plus ≤100 MB KV. Honest total **~4.40 GB/token → 54.3 GB/s = 35.6% of the 152.6 ceiling**, not
   33.5%. This *strengthens* the campaign thesis: still ~36% of ceiling, so the deficit is latency/sync,
   not bytes. (NON-CLAIM — arithmetic, not a counter.)

### Both hypotheses refuted structurally, without spending a lock window

- `GGML_MMID_SLAB` partitions `n_groups × ne01` rows where `n_groups` is the distinct-used-expert count, and
  only when `cne1 == 1` (`ggml-cpu.c:1840-1900`). It changes *which thread reads which run*, not the row
  set; both methods give 1.2964 GB identically.
- The **PLE table is off by three orders of magnitude**: one gather site (`ple.layers=[1]`), 16 rows × 90 B
  = **1.44 KB/token**. Even charging a full 2 MB THP per row gives 32 MB, and DRAM is 64 B-line granular so
  the real cost is ~2 KB. **The 28.8 GB PLE table is a TLB/latency object, not a bandwidth object** — which
  explains B7's null from a second, independent direction.

### Levers it hands us, both already on the board

- **B4** — quantise the F32 router: 251.7 → 62.9 MB = 4.5% of the token, *and* it hits the worst straggler
  in the graph (those 48 nodes take 4.197 ms wall for 2.102 ms of compute, and contain the token's single
  worst node at 1118 µs wall for 32 µs compute). Bytes and imbalance in one change.
- **D6** — `GET_ROWS` is unconditionally single-task, moving 113 MB at 13.1 GB/s on one core for **8.4 ms,
  8.7% of the token**.

## B10 — reduced-vocabulary drafting: NO-GO, and the reason is L3

Measured on an own `-DGGML_CPU_PROF` build of `10221`/`c51e4dabf`, knob proven compiled in, two
region-locked arms, placement 0.1% dev, both outputs COHERENT at a 72-token production prompt.

| | ms/call | effective GB/s |
|---|---|---|
| `lm_head`, trunk graph (plain) | 3.742 | 139 — 94% of the 153 ceiling |
| `lm_head`, MTP draft graph | ~2.0 wall / 1.55 compute | **260 — 1.7× the DRAM ceiling** |

**260 GB/s cannot come from DRAM.** The MTP graph is only 144 nodes and ~105 MB of other weights, so the
521 MB head substantially survives in this box's **384 MiB L3** between draft steps. Derived two independent
ways from the same arm (386 evals = 78 trunk + 308 MTP; 78 MTP evals are `process()` catch-ups adding with
`logits=0`, streaming no head bytes — confirmed by `dst ne[1]=0`).

**So the head is ~4–5% of the 43.17 ms token, not the 8.6% the byte model predicts.** A K=65,536 slice
ceilings at +3.0–3.9%; even a *free* head is only +5%. Upstream #25187 independently measured +1.4%.

**The α side, zero compute**: rebuilding the model's BPE from the GGUF and tokenising all 97,858 tokens of
COHERENT `speed-claim` output shows a contiguous id-prefix 64k slice — the only variant needing no new
artifact and no remap — covers **97.95%**, because **Qwen's vocab is not frequency-ordered above 65k**
(`'```'` = 71093, `'.**'` = 159029). Δα ≈ −0.017 ≈ −2.6 to −3.4%: **exactly break-even.**

Everything that could have blocked it structurally turned out fine — correctness is output-lossless
(`server-context.cpp:3876` compares the target's sampled id, so an out-of-slice truth is a rejected draft,
never a wrong accepted token); the shared head is safe to alias read-only from the MTP graph; q6_K rows are
2100 B exactly, so a row slice is a contiguous byte prefix needing no requantisation. **The lever simply
isn't big enough**, and the arithmetic that made it look big was mine.

### Two corrections to me

1. My relayed break-even (`α′ ≥ 0.571` from `tokens/step = 1+α`) was wrong. With `--spec-draft-n-max 4`
   running autoregressively on the single head, tokens/step is `Σα^i ≈ 3.79`. Sensitivity is ~1.5–2.0%
   throughput per 0.01 α and break-even is **α′ ≈ 0.79**. I had loosened a constraint that was closer to
   right before I touched it.
2. **The transferable finding: the campaign's byte-based roofline over-prices draft-side weights by ~2×.**
   Cost future "shrink the drafter" levers against the measured **260 GB/s** effective rate, not 153 GB/s.
   This is the second time in two days that a byte model mispriced something (cf. B11's PLE table, off by
   three orders of magnitude) — bytes are a poor proxy once a tensor fits in 384 MiB of L3.

**Cheapest remaining probe in this family, no code required** (candidate, not filed): requantise the head to
IQ4_XS into a new MTP GGUF's `blk.48.nextn.shared_head_head`, which the graph already prefers — 521 → 338 MB,
expected +1.4–1.8%.
