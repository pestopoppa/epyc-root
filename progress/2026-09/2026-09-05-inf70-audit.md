## Axis S reconciled: the barrier budget is gross, not recoverable in bulk

The contradiction — 2,478 ns/node measured on two independent instruments, pricing 5,410 barriers at ~13 ms,
against D1 removing 940 barriers bit-exact for −0.05 ± 0.5 ms — resolves into one mechanism:

> The ~2.2–2.5 µs is not a fixed toll per barrier. It is the **congestion** cost of 48 cores doing a
> simultaneous atomic RMW on one cache line plus the release round-trip, paid in full **only when threads
> arrive together**. After staggered work the late arriver finds 47 threads already spinning and the marginal
> cost collapses to release latency alone. The wait that remains is **imbalance, not the barrier** — it would
> persist if the barrier were deleted, because the consumer needs all the data anyway.

This predicts the whole record with no special pleading: D1's null (those 940 barriers sit right after
staggered matmul work), INF-10's −50 barriers, the hierarchical-barrier prototype's flatness — *and* predicts
that tiny-op chains do pay, since every thread arrives simultaneously with nothing to do.

**So Axis S as I framed it was too broad.** The target is not 5,410 barriers but "barriers at nodes where
threads arrive together": SYNC-2's tiny-op set (~3.6 ms) plus D2(i)'s 579 elision candidates (~1.3 ms),
against an 84.2 ms token. The rest of the dead time is imbalance, and the larger money is placement (D6 ~15
ms, B2 ~11 ms). I have lifted the hold on SYNC-2 and told it to size its claim against 3.6 ms, not 12.

**The estimator survived a check I asked for, and the reason matters.**
`ggml_compute_forward_scale_f32` splits over rows; every decode SCALE is `ne = [2560,1,1,1]`, so `nr = 1` and
only `ith = 0` gets work while threads 1–47 enter, do nothing, and park. The 375 SCALE nodes are genuinely
single-task **at the kernel, not the planner** — so the advisory-`n_tasks` finding does not invalidate them.
Worth noting the discipline: I asked whether a source-level claim survived an execution-level correction, and
it did, but for a different reason than the one originally given.

Falsification is one run of SYNC-1's per-(node,thread) instrument: residual ≈2.2 µs on tiny/single-task nodes
and a few hundred ns after heavy nodes confirms the model; ~2.2 µs uniformly refutes it.

## SYNC-8 filed: two latent heap overflows

Neither is reachable from qwen4exp decode; both are real for other graphs and probably upstream.

- **`SET_ROWS`** — planner sizes wdata as `ne0 * n_tasks` with `n_tasks = 1`, kernel indexes
  `wdata + (nc + CACHE_LINE_SIZE_F32) * ith` for `ith` up to 47 (`ops.cpp:5457`). **Any quantised KV cache
  with an F16 source overruns the work buffer**; the sizing also omits the padding the kernel adds. The same
  `n_tasks = 1` carries a comment claiming serialisation prevents a write race — **that guard is inert**,
  because the kernel row-splits with nth=48. We are safe only because `nr` is 1–4 there.
- **`SOFT_MAX`** — `n_tasks = MIN(n_threads, nrows)` = 1 at single-row decode, kernel indexes wdata by `ith`
  up to 47 (`ops.cpp:5802`). qwen4exp uses FLASH_ATTN_EXT and has no SOFT_MAX nodes. `ROPE` is fine.

**General rule: every `n_tasks = 1` case that also sizes wdata by `n_tasks` is a candidate.** Filed as a
sweep for the pattern rather than two point fixes.

SYNC-1 pre-registered this before its confirming run, which is the right order and worth recording as such.

`node_872` is a MUL_MAT `iq4_nl [320,10240]` with **97 same-shape siblings that compute in 47–59 µs**. It

**This is my error more than anyone's**: I promoted those two nodes to "~1.4 ms/token, likely the more

If confirmed: ~1.4 ms leaves the envelope and MUL_MAT's pre-D8 dead drops 7.19 → 6.10 ms. It sharpens the

## SYNC-3 NO-GO — and the census is 26% wrong on the actual base

Re-censused at `c51e4dabf`: **graph wall/token is 76.371 ms, not 96.267.** `GATED_DELTA_NET` is
**0.510 ms wall / 0.071 dead (14.0%)**, not 2.999/2.214 (73.8%). The state CPY nodes are 0.171 ms wall. The
brief's "~4.4 ms dead in the two worst ops" is **0.19 ms**. Three token figures have now circulated today
(96.3 / 84.2 / 76.4) and only a measurement on one's own base is safe — I should have required that in the
first brief instead of handing four agents a number I had not verified was on their history.

**The load-bearing finding: the recurrent state is cache-resident.** It moves three times — 678 MB/token —
in 2.922 ms, an effective **234 GB/s, 1.5× the DRAM ceiling**, with individual 3.0 MiB CPY nodes at ~1.2 TB/s
aggregate. So B11's "226 MB/token omitted from the ledger" is arithmetically true and economically
irrelevant, and eliminating copies of it buys nearly nothing.

**That is the third cache-residency finding today** — draft head 260 GB/s (B10), GDN state 234 GB/s (SYNC-3),
PLE table 1.44 KB/token (B11). The pattern is now unmistakable: **a byte ledger is systematically wrong on
this box for anything re-read per token that fits in 384 MiB of L3.** Every byte-based lever this campaign
has priced has come in under estimate for the same reason.

**Mechanism, named from source**: GDN is neither single-tasked nor state-serialized. `nr = H × n_seqs = 48`
against `nth = 48` gives exactly one head per thread, `dr = 1`, work-stealing never steals, and the wall is

## B12: my residency hypothesis is refuted and inverted — the shared head was already optimal

## SYNC-12: the entry point exists, and CUDA's `K<=1` bail is scope, not soundness

**The safety question is answered from the code, and SYNC-3's behaviour is safe rather than lucky.** The CUDA
*kernel* at K==1 is already the fused form; only the *matcher* refuses. `gated_delta_net.cu:329` sets
`state_d = cache->data` **unconditionally on K**, and the `if constexpr (!keep_rs_t)` epilogue writes the
final state straight into the cache slot at an already-advanced offset — code exercised on every non-rollback
GPU decode. Two tells that the author considered K==1 and dropped it: `slot_stride` is documented as *"0 when
K==1"*, and the `K > 1 ? … : 0` ternary in `ggml-cuda.cu` is **dead code** under the early return above it.
It is gated off because the K==1 destination is a `ggml_view_2d` against the K>1 `ggml_view_3d` the matcher
assumes — a second shape to validate for **zero GPU payoff**.

The real soundness question turned out to be **aliasing**, and it resolves identically at both K: `src[5]`
comes from a `GET_ROWS` gather, never the cache tensor, so read source and write target are disjoint. The new
pass checks that explicitly — **one check more than CUDA's own matcher makes.**

**`ggml_cpu_try_fuse_ops()` already existed** with CUDA's exact shape and one hardcoded clause; it is now a
registry. **Backend safety is structural rather than defensive** — the pass lives only in `ggml-cpu.c` and
never mutates the graph, so other backends see a byte-identical *unfused* graph. That is the third of the
three options I offered and the best of them: **the fused form is unrepresentable for a backend that cannot
execute it**, so there are no asserts to remember in three files and no capability check to get wrong.

Eight streams byte-identical over 288 tokens, with hit counts proving the pass fires (`gdn_state_cache` =
10368 = 36 layers × 288 tokens **exactly**). `EXP`/`EXPM1` failures are pre-existing — they fail under
`GGML_CPU_DISABLE_FUSION=1` too, so no pass can reach them.

**It also declined to claim something it had started**: the full `test-backend-ops -b CPU` sweep was killed
(PID verified dead) because it was running **unlocked while siblings were timing 1% effects**. Owed and
stated, not claimed.

### The second consumer found the trap in SYNC-9

Nine lines, implemented rather than sketched, bit-identical, **62,999 elisions per process**. And the insight
that matters: those nodes' `ggml_compute_forward` is **already a no-op**, so their 0.173 ms is **pure
barrier** — **eliding the node without also eliding the barrier recovers nothing.** A naive early-return
would have measured zero and been read as "node removal does not convert", when in fact no barrier would have
been removed. Given SYNC-1's one-barrier-per-node identity, `skip_barrier` is the entire experiment.

I have told SYNC-2 to take the code rather than reimplement it, and to **verify `skip_barrier` is set before
believing any null.** That single warning may have saved the campaign's decisive experiment from producing a
confidently wrong answer.
