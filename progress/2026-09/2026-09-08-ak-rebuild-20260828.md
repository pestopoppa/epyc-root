### 3. The gate, and the framing the operator corrected mid-task

**The gate (binding):** the sweep **must not run until after the operator's planned host reboot, at which
they will make BIOS changes**. The **contents of those changes are not recorded here** — whoever runs the
post-reboot work must get them from the operator. C8 (`:280`) carries a *prepared proposal* (5600 MT/s,

**★ This entry records a correction the operator made to the framing while the task was in flight, so the
record shows the correction and not only the corrected text.** The brief as first relayed said a BIOS change
is an **era boundary** that voids every pre-reboot number, and that today's champion figures "may not be
compared across the boundary without re-measurement". **That over-claims, and the operator struck it:**

> *"just because the instrument era changes doesn't mean we can't compare numbers. Performance speed is an
> absolute metric which we're trying to maximize. As long as it improves, it unlocks new performance
> regardless of the instrument era. The issue would appear if, after making the BIOS changes, the
> performance numbers were to regress."*

So the filed task (**R23-65**) distinguishes what the first framing conflated:

- **Absolute headlines are comparable across the boundary.** t/s is a physical quantity, not an
  instrument-relative score. Today's figures (CPU **27.893 / 43.281**; GPU 27B **79.25 / 179.12**, 35B
  **112.68 / 310.96**) are the **pre-BIOS reference point**, and the post-reboot re-measurement is compared
  against them **directly, with no era caveat**. The operational rule is an **asymmetry: improvement is a
  genuine gain; a REGRESSION is the signal to investigate the BIOS change.** `MEASUREMENT.md` §6 is **not**
  cited as forbidding comparison of absolute magnitudes, because it does not.
- **Attribution needs care, as the ordinary two-conditions-at-once confound** — the same one as R23-62's
  champion divergence, not an era-labelling rule. Change one thing across the reboot, or say the delta is
  unattributed.
- **Floors need recalibration on their own merit**, because a floor describes **dispersion**, not level,
  and dispersion is what gates A/B decisions. If BIOS moves the variance, a stale floor mis-gates in both
  directions — too tight calls noise decisive, too loose refuses a real effect. That justification is
  independent of the absolute-metric point.

**R23-65** therefore recalibrates every floor post-reboot (CPU launch 0.609% / 0.356%; arm 0.501%;
between-session 2.793%; GPU serving standing 4.581% `unverified` and today's 6.596% n=24; the 7.223%
contaminated A/A), at n ≥ 24 where it gates, recording `n` and an interval.
