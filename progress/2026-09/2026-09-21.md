### The global cap is gone, and the single-node arm is what proves it

- One node now reads **124.5 GB/s of a 134.4 GB/s** three-channel theoretical = **93% of channel limit**
  (pre-BIOS: 66 of 115.2 = 57%).
- Four nodes streaming together reach **446.8 = 90% of 4 x 124.5**. Pre-BIOS, C0-c measured each node
  collapsing from 66 GB/s alone to ~40 GB/s when all four streamed (171 GB/s aggregate). **That
  collapse no longer happens.**
- So this is NOT merely faster DIMMs. 4800 -> 5600 is worth +17% on its own; the measured effect is
  +170%. The **uncore serialisation** that C0-c identified as a global ~170 GB/s cap has been removed.
  Enabling memory interleave is the plausible carrier; the clock alone cannot produce this.

### Roofline consequence (computed, not measured)

At the ledger's 4.16 GB streamed per token, the bandwidth-only floor moves from **~27 ms/token
(37 t/s)** to **~10 ms/token (~99 t/s)**. Decode will NOT move 2.7x: the ledger puts only ~27 ms of a
~97 ms token on bandwidth, and the ~70 ms dispatch floor does not move with BIOS. Prediction recorded
before measuring, so C5 can falsify it.

### The prediction I recorded before the run was WRONG, and the ledger row it came from is wrong too
