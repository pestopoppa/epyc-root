## Speculative decoding: wired, measured, profitable (INF-77 DS41-B13)

Serving class, -t 48 -tb 96, same server config throughout:

| arm | decode t/s |
|---|---|
| no drafter | 11.43 |
| greedy, serial verify | 7.18 |
| **greedy, batched verify** | **17.85 median; 18.79-22.08 on low-entropy** |

The serial path (forced for DSpark at temp <= 0) decodes each accepted token in its own
`llama_decode`, so it yields exactly 1 token per target decode and **can never exceed 1x** — a
drafter under it is worse than no drafter. Operator adopted batched greedy; recipe default is now
`greedy-batched` at block 2 (research `89e7f210`).

**Corrections I had to make to my own reporting, both caught before they hardened:**
1. I compared llama-bench tg128 (13.18) against a serving run (6.01). Different instrument classes;
   the comparison is refused, not caveated. The same-class control is 8.21-11.43.
2. I reported divergence as "confined to high-entropy prompts". The two low-entropy prompts had
   returned **1 token then EOS, empty content, in every arm** — they were instruction-phrased and
   this model ships no chat template, so they proved nothing and I read their silence as evidence.
   The re-run with continuation prompts: the arithmetic chain forks into a SYNONYM with every number
   preserved; the table and the counting sequence are identical over 160 tokens. Divergence tracks
   near-ties, not entropy class.
3. I quoted 22% of a 460.8 GB/s roofline. That is the sequential-stream figure; decode is a gather
   with dequant, and this host's measured gemv-pattern ceiling is 153-167 GB/s. We are at ~61% of
   the ceiling that applies, not 22% of one that does not.

measured against it is comparing to a degraded baseline — a measurement-validity hazard, not just a

## Measured: host READ bandwidth ceiling is 220 GB/s (not 153–167, not 460.8)

`readbw` (`/workspace/tmp/readbw.c`, binary `/mnt/raid0/llm/tmp/readbw`): pure sequential read,
OpenMP, parallel first-touch, NUMA-interleaved, 16–24 GiB buffer (far beyond the 384 MB L3), at the
recipe's own thread counts and taskset: **48 threads 220.2 GB/s, 96 threads 220.8 GB/s.** This is
the ceiling that applies to weight streaming. The earlier 153–167 GB/s figures are the
*gemv-pattern* (gather + dequant) numbers and 212 GB/s is copy (read+write); 460.8 is theoretical.
Against 220: dense `MUL_MAT` streams at 176 GB/s (80%), experts at 199 GB/s (90%) — the whole
streaming-efficiency prize is ~7 ms of an 86 ms token, so **bytes are the lever** (the dense path
holds 59% of bytes at 8.5 bpw). Read BW is flat 48→96 threads, which independently explains the
flat decode thread curve. Full derivation: `/mnt/raid0/llm/tmp/ds41-hypotheses/HYPOTHESES.md`
(rev2), condensed into the campaign inbox brief.
