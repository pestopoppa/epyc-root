## B7 ANSWERED — is the PLE table under-quantized for this machine? NO.

Pristine `c51e4dabf`, `GGML_IQK=1` (production kernel), 40 chunks, placement proven both passes:

```
Mean ln(PPL(Q)/PPL(base)) : 0.004707 ± 0.004380   t = +1.07σ   NOT significant
95% CI on PPL ratio       : [-0.39%, +1.34%]      MDE = 1.235% PPL
Mean KLD                  : 0.064860 ± 0.001713   t = 37.9σ
Same top-1                : 92.020 ± 0.268 %      RMS Δp 8.27%
```

Doubling PLE precision (IQ4_NL → Q8_0) buys no quality; the sign runs slightly against it. **This is a null
with a floor, not an absence of measurement** — the same run resolved the distributional change at 38σ, so
the instrument is demonstrably not blind. Pairing gave 18× tighter resolution than raw PPLs (Cor 98.06%).

**BF16 is not worth a second pass.** Q8_0 nearly doubled precision for zero gain against a 1.235% floor;
BF16 would cost +25.6 GB resident to chase a gain bounded below that floor. B7 closes with no successor.

**Correction to the structural argument I used to pre-judge this.** I argued twice that the PLE row is
diluted — input to two GEMVs averaged over 2560 terms, `ple.layers=[1]` (1 of 48), projections themselves
IQ4_XS. That is wrong: ~8% of top-1 tokens flip, because the `key` branch collapses to a scalar through a
sigmoid gate that multiplies the entire value vector. PLE sensitivity is HIGH. The conclusion survived for a
different reason than the one I gave — **sensitivity is not headroom**. Note what this implies about method:
a greedy-identity gate would have reported a large difference and taught us nothing, since 8% of tokens
change without quality changing. Only the paired KLD/PPL separates "changed" from "improved".

## Claim-grade ABA, depth, and KV quant — three results, two of them overturns

25 arms × 24 production prompts = 600 requests; every arm 20 COHERENT + 4 SHORT, zero garbage.

### 1. The 23.62 headline does not reproduce. Quote 23.16 t/s / 1.876×.

ABA (A-plain → B-config, ×3) in ONE lock window: **B = 23.16 t/s** (sd 0.14, spread 1.1%, range 23.01–23.26),
**A = 12.35 t/s** (sd 0.24), **ratio 1.876×**; per-round 1.845 / 1.886 / 1.897, B faster on 20/20 prompts every
round. be1-ship's 23.623 sits 2.0% above the mean and 2.6 round-sd out; no round reached 23.4.

The configuration is **confirmed** and the mechanism reproduces exactly — α 0.8274, drafted/token 0.8900,
identical to 4 dp across all seven n-max-4 arms. What failed was the number, not the finding: 23.623 was the
optimistic edge of a single unreplicated arm. This is why the arm was labelled NON-CLAIM at publication.

### 2. Depth beyond n-max 4 does not pay — and my earlier sweep was a drift artefact.

The cross-window sweep suggesting n5/n6/n8 beat n4 by ~3% was wrong. A same-window ALTERNATING n4-vs-n5 test
returns **ratio 0.9987**, 12/20 and 10/20 prompts — coin flips. p_min 0.5 vs 0.6 is flat. The plateau is real;
no better operating point exists.

### 3. Do NOT quantise the KV cache under MTP. Ship f16. (B9 closed.)

Size analysis was correct and irrelevant (12 of 48 layers carry KV; 24.0 KiB/token = 2.42% of budget; 45–90 MiB
saved). The **sign was wrong for MTP**: plain gains +0.7% to +2.1% as predicted, but MTP **loses 2.5–3.5%**
because α falls 0.8274 → 0.8166. `attn_rot_k/v` did flip to 1 (head dim 256) on both the main and DSA indexer
caches, and that never-exercised path is clean — 96 quantised-KV requests, zero incoherence, no assert.
`LLAMA_ATTN_ROT_DISABLE=1` was not a rescue but was decisive as **attribution**: it recovers 1.9% and lifts α to
**0.8329, above f16's own 0.8274**. So the **Hadamard rotation, not quantisation error, costs the acceptance**;
q8_0 still loses 1.4% with rotation off. **A KV-quant win on a plain decoder does not transfer to a speculative
one** — the metric that pays is α, not bytes.

### The standing rule this batch earned

**The host drifts ~3% over hours** — same config, 23.16 at 12:52 vs 22.58–22.73 at 15:05–15:55 — while repeating
to **1.1% within a window**. Therefore **any INF-70 comparison below ~5% must be same-window and alternating, or
it is not evidence.** Both overturns above were cross-window artefacts of exactly that magnitude, and
be1-ship's 18 arms ran sequentially over ~4 hours: a sequential arm matrix measures drift as if it were the
treatment.

### Bandwidth: MTP takes CPU decode off the bandwidth wall

Plain 51.3 GB/s (33.5% of 153). MTP's *plain-equivalent* 96.3 GB/s (62.9%) is not what it moves — the verify
batch carries **3.79 tokens per forward**, so real traffic is **1.096 GB/token = 25.4 GB/s (16.6%)**. Under
speculation the lever is amortising the weight read across accepted tokens, not raising GB/s.

### Contention attribution

`ps %CPU` is cumulative and showed idle agent processes at 40–50%. A delta-based sampler plus
`Cpus_allowed_list` gave the truth: the `autokernel` `llama-bench` pinned to 184-191 is a **GPU peer, not
contention** (confirming the earlier retraction), while a peer `test-backend-ops` at `0-191` taking 1.7–3.3 live
cores **is**, and it landed on the A3 control.
