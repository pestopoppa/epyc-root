## MEAS-1 — filed, UNDECIDED, needs an operator call. **The options changed twice today.**

### The framing that decides it is a HARDWARE fact, not a scheduling one

This is a **96-physical-core box presenting 192 logical CPUs**. Every logical CPU in 96-191 is the SMT
sibling of one in 0-95 (96↔0 … 191↔95, **exhaustive**, verified directly against `thread_siblings_list`
for all 192). Any second tenant doing real work is *necessarily* on the bench cores' siblings.
**On this host, isolation between two CPU-heavy campaigns is not achievable by placement at all — only
by time.** That converts MEAS-1 from "should we tidy up our tooling" into **"two campaigns cannot both
run CPU-heavy work and both trust their sub-5% numbers"**, which is the actual decision.

**The originally filed "fence via cgroup/cpuset" option therefore CANNOT BE BUILT and has been removed
from the package** rather than left standing as an apparent alternative. It was challenged by the
autokernel session and the challenge was correct.

### Evidence, corrected

- **The dominant contention is BUILDS, not bench threads.** autokernel's `gates.compiles` runs `jobs=64`
  pinned to `cpu_list="96-183"` — the sibling set of **88 of our 96** bench cores — and its bench on
  `184-191` covers the other 8: **96 of 96**. Sampled live during their run 30 while we were measuring:
  **9 concurrent `cc1plus` at 100% CPU each.** A far better candidate for our 16.8% A/A spread than the 8
  GPU host threads originally flagged.
- **★ The discriminator is the PEAK, not the total foreign load** — HARNESS-1's three A/A arms (same
  binary, same config, **bit-identical output**, purely timing):

  | arm | tw t/s | foreign CPU p50 | foreign CPU max | heaviest foreign |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---|
  | `A_OLD1` | 19.313 | 399.9 | **3218.0** | `cc1plus` 1200, `test-backend-ops` 363 |
  | `A_OLD2` | 22.860 | 621.2 | 1214.6 | `python` 840 |
  | `A_OLD3` | 22.421 | 477.8 | 1458.0 | `python` 600 |

  **All three: 16.47% spread. Excluding the compiler-contended arm: 1.94%.** `A_OLD1` has the *lowest*
  median foreign CPU yet is 15% slower — unpinned noise (`ps`, `htop`) dominates the median and moves
  nothing; what hurts is a **burst of 3218% = 32 cores, a third of the bench region.** So (A) need only
  cover **builds**, which is a much cheaper fix than the package first implied.
- **The `loadavg < 10` PRE-LOAD gate cannot protect an arm** — it passes, then the burst arrives *during*
  the measurement. Only **in-window** sampling catches it, which is why the sampler's `184-191` mislabel
  (WRAP-4) mattered as much as it did.
- **Attribution recorded AS THE PEER MADE IT — shared suspicion, not settled on them.** The `cc1plus` we
  caught at 1200% was **unpinned** (`0-191`) while theirs are pinned to `96-183`, so the process we
  sampled was most likely **one of our own** unlocked builds. Their defect is still real
  (pinned-but-unlocked contends whenever a build overlaps an arm, just on a predictable 88 cores). **Two
  separate defects.** A peer correcting their own overreach is worth preserving accurately.
- **Theirs alone, no decision needed:** their `serving.py` pins nothing — the `llama-server` at their
  serving gate has no `taskset` in its Popen argv, so it can land **directly on 0-95**. They are fixing
  it. Separately they have made `llama-server` pinnable (`Recipe.cpu_list`) but are **deliberately
  leaving the default `None`**: setting it changes the measured condition and would invalidate their
  3.536% serving floor, so the pin and its re-calibration must land in one window.

### The decision package (three options; fencing is not among them)

- **(A) SERIALIZE** — heavy tooling takes the region lock and queues against bench arms. **Serializing
  compiles alone plausibly takes the A/A floor from 16.47% to ~1.94%**: the difference between a 3–5%
  effect being unresolvable and being measurable. Costs the other campaigns throughput directly.
- **(B) PUBLISH A SCHEDULE** — heavy work advertises its windows; sub-5% timing avoids them. Cheaper,
  weaker, relies on compliance.
- **(C) ACCEPT AND REGRESS** — measure through the noise, log foreign load per arm, treat it as a known
  variance term. No host change; prices the confound rather than removing it, and cannot rescue runs
  already taken.

**No recommendation is offered yet, deliberately** — SYNC-19/20's numbers under a quiet window come
first.

### Two couplings the operator must not miss

1. **MEAS-1 and autokernel's `R23-49` / their `OP-39` are ONE decision.** They queued the identical
   tradeoff, cross-referencing ours, because (A) costs **them** throughput while it buys **us**
   resolution. **Neither session can decide it alone.** Ruled on separately, one campaign's answer
   silently sets the other's default. (Distinct from INF-70's own resolved OP-39.)
2. **The quiet window is REQUESTED, NOT ARRANGED — do not record it as secured.** autokernel accepted the
   protocol (hold run 30 at a task boundary when SYNC-19/20 acquire) but **cannot deliver it
   unilaterally**: run starts and stops are operator-gated on their side. **On current evidence
   SYNC-19/20 may run CONTENDED, in which case their headline is a NON-CLAIM — decided now, in advance,
   not after the numbers land.**

### Two tasks filed off it

- **MEAS-2 — adopt `build_locked.sh` as the standing build idiom and promote it out of scratch.** The
  bypass is **systemic**: an audit of every INF-70 build script found **19 of 21 take no region lock at
  all** (only `harness1/build.sh` and `b7-ple/build.sh` do). `champion3/build3.sh` was merely the one we
  caught — patching it alone would have fixed nothing and manufactured false closure. The wrapper at
  `/mnt/raid0/llm/tmp/inf70/build_locked.sh` runs `cmake --build` under `region-lock` with
  `--role build --timeout-s 0` (fair queue, never polls). **Verified to actually exclude rather than
  merely appear to**: the occupancy registry computes blockers from **region overlap regardless of role**
  (`cpu_region_lock.py`, the `overlaps` set) — role is attribution only, and only a `shared=True`
  same-role request joins a cohort. Checked deliberately, because the CLI help calls it a "per-role lock"
  and the lock *files* are per-role, which would have made a role-labelled wrapper **vacuous** had the
  cross-role mutex not existed. *Explicit decline: the 19 scratch scripts are NOT being edited — they are
  spent one-shot artifacts; the value is the convention and PROD-1 importing it.*
- **MEAS-3 — retention row: `/mnt/raid0/llm/tmp/inf70/agents/sync16/` is a CROSS-CAMPAIGN DEPENDENCY, do
  not reclaim.** Marked in place at `agents/sync16/DO-NOT-RECLAIM.md`. The autokernel session is reusing
  its **sibling-expanded `cpus_allowed` residency check** in their serving gate and filed that path as
  the source; the directory also holds the **pre-registered A/A control that caught SYNC-16's build
  artefact** and the long-stream identity harness. It looks exactly like spent scratch and is not.
