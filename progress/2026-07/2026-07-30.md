## NUMA placement defect — two production CPU roles serving at ~half speed

New handoff: [`handoffs/active/numa-placement-defect-20260730.md`](../../handoffs/active/numa-placement-defect-20260730.md)
(master-index row **N24**). Owning suspension banner stays in
[`handoffs/active/batched-decode-measurement.md`](../../handoffs/active/batched-decode-measurement.md).
All figures measured today on `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MTP-Q8_0` unless noted; era
`production-consolidated-v8` @ `67a433bf4` (binary `10107`); NPS4 host; region-lock held on
`q0..q3` as `role='bench'`. Observation-grade (`n=1-2` reps), not promotable.

Five defects — three in **live production wiring**, two in the E5 harness:

- **D1 — straddling cpusets with no NUMA policy.** `stack_numa.py`'s `NUMA_NODE0 = "0-47,96-143"`
  and `NUMA_NODE1 = "48-95,144-191"` are NPS2-era names; on this NPS4 host each spans **two**
  nodes. `frontdoor` (8070) and `ingest_long_context` (8085) launch there with **no `numactl`**,
  so weights first-touch onto one node and half the thread team reads cross-node.
  `llama-bench` tg128: straddle/no-interleave **10.83 ± 0.04 (r=10)**, straddle+interleave cold
  15.35, **full machine `0-95` + `interleave=all` 23.36 ± 0.11 (r=10)** — a **2.16×** loss, i.e.
  the role serves at **46%** of canonical. `--interleave` binds at FIRST TOUCH only, hence a
  silent no-op against a warm cache: that is why the defect survived casual re-testing.

  *Corrected 2026-07-30: an earlier draft of this entry claimed 3.0× / "~1/3 speed". That
  divided by the WARM arm `7.81 ± 3.82`, whose ±49% error bar made it the noisiest cell
  measured; the r=10 re-measurement gives `10.83 ± 0.04`, agreeing with the cold arm. The
  live losses are 2.16× (`frontdoor`) and 1.85× (`ingest_long_context`).*
  `eval_batch_frontdoor` (18070), `architect_general` (8083) and the `worker_general` full
  instance (8072) are full-machine/interleaved and unaffected. Instrument validated: single
  NPS4 node measured 8.80 vs registry 8.90 (1% match); v7 and v8 identical at 9.76.
- **D2 — shared mmap means only one instance can be node-local.** llama.cpp mmaps the GGUF, so
  pages sit in the shared page cache and are placed **once**, by whichever process faults them
  first; later instances inherit it regardless of their own `--membind`. Four `--membind`
  quarters, from `/proc/<pid>/numa_maps`: mmap = 25.6 / 25.6 / 24.2 / 26.9 % local (30 GB host
  RAM) vs `--no-mmap` = 100 % on all four (171 GB). Fleet decode (4×quarter, np=1, MTP on):
  **40.91 → 52.13 tok/s, +27%**, at **+141 GB** RAM. **Corollary: fleet throughput is
  nondeterministic across reboots** — it depends on instance START ORDER. E5 W1 had one
  straddling instance load first and put 100% of pages on node0 (all 11 later cells inherited
  it); today's simultaneous quad load gave each ~25%.
- **D3 — SMT oversubscription.** Halves are wired `-t 96` onto 48 physical cores. `-t 48` gives
  **+13% per-stream / +8.5% aggregate** at np=4 (13.49 vs 11.91 tok/s). Quarters same sign,
  smaller (+2.4%).
- **D4 — E5 metric was wall-clock, not decode.** `aggregate_predicted_tps` divided by
  `wall_seconds` (model load + prefill + idle included). Renamed **`aggregate_wallclock_tps`**,
  true **`aggregate_decode_tps`** added. Offline re-derivation from persisted `requests.jsonl`
  shows this bug alone understated throughput by only **~5% median** (22% worst, at T=1) —
  **not** the main story; D1/D2 are.
- **D5 — E5 rungs never reached nominal concurrency.** Achieved vs nominal T: 100% @T=1, 86%
  @T=4, 80% @T=8, 77% @T=16, **47% @T=32**. The fixed 43-prompt batch drains before saturating,
  so the high-T rungs measured a partially drained queue.
