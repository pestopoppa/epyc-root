## P2 — New instruments (both address named, dated gaps)

- [ ] **ID-7 — Add an `ordered_subsequence` verifier to the canonical `answer_scoring` library.** From arXiv **2506.15629** (Sakai et al., **ACL 2025 Main — peer-reviewed**, credibility 4): give an ordered concept list, lemmatize the completion, check all concepts appear as an ordered subsequence; return graded coverage in [0,1] plus a binary all-in-order flag. Why it matters here: our 43-question `instruction_precision` suite has **no ordering/sequence verifier of any kind** (verified against chapter 07's full verifier enumeration), and the best model in the paper reaches only **~75%** ordered rate against ~99% unordered coverage — an IF axis with real headroom where ours is saturated. **Add BOTH metrics, not one** — my earlier note that Ordered Rate and Coverage-with-order are redundant is **overturned**: they diverge by up to **26.5 pts** on weak models (Qwen2-0.5B 30.84 vs 57.34) and converge only at 405B, i.e. they carry distinct signal exactly in the small/quantized regime we care about. Source is ACL 2025 Main **and an Outstanding Paper**. Must land in the canonical library, not as an 11th bespoke scorer. **Confound to record**: the paper's leaderboard runs 32 open models at 4-bit against full-precision API arms — do not cite that table itself as quant-quality evidence. Known fragility: spaCy-lemmatization dependence (same class as the substring/comma brittleness we've been bitten by).
  - Owner: [`scoring-infra-standardization.md`](scoring-infra-standardization.md); consumers [`eval-tower-verification.md`](eval-tower-verification.md), [`architect-model-selection-bench.md`](architect-model-selection-bench.md).

- [ ] **ID-8 — FrontierCS floor probe (~10 problems, one production arm) before any harness spend.** arXiv **2512.15699**, 156 open-ended problems with per-problem evaluators, open-source. EV-9 records that our production suites sit at 90–94% and "cannot resolve" the top-2 models; `architect-model-selection-bench.md` just burned a cycle on `olympiadbench_numeric` returning a well-powered null (A1 89.3 / A3 88.0 / A4 89.3, all p ≥ 0.77). FrontierCS is maximally non-saturating (frontier ~29.4 vs human 95.41). **Symmetric risk, stated up front**: all published baselines are proprietary API models; our open-weight arms may land near zero, trading a saturation problem for an equally non-discriminative *floor* problem. The floor probe settles that cheaply. Also requires a sandboxed exec path we don't have (same blocker as LiveCodeBench).
  - Owner: [`eval-tower-verification.md`](eval-tower-verification.md) (EV-9).

- [ ] **ID-9 — Evaluate Behavior-Conditioned Inference as a Tier-1 reasoning-compression lever.** arXiv **2509.13237**: extract named procedural "behaviors" from our own successful traces, retrieve k into context; reports up to **46% output-token reduction at matched accuracy** — above conciseness prompting (37%) and Soft Thinking (22.4%). Zero-training, inference-time only. **Corrected**: the 46% is **"up to"**, output-tokens-only, with no accuracy figure at that operating point, and the **input-side cost is admitted but never measured** (`"involves a larger number of input tokens due to the inclusion of retrieved behaviors"`; **k=40** behaviors retrieved per query on AIME). Best-instrumented paper in the sweep (5 seeds on MATH, 80 runs on AIME, production-style temp 0.6/top-p 0.95).

  - Score **total pipeline tokens** (retrieved-behavior prefill + decode), never decode alone.
  - Owner: [`reasoning-compression.md`](reasoning-compression.md).
