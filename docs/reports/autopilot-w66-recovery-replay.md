The primary rule permits one advisory at a completed assistant turn, with at least 120 seconds left in the original phase. It requires either four occurrences of the same normalized tool input and unchanged observed result in the last 32 inspection results after at least 20 consecutive inspections, or 80 consecutive inspection results. An out-of-scope file change such as a cache write cannot count as repair. An in-scope snapshot change, a missing snapshot, a check or a non-inspection call resets the streak. The two CPU PFXC-A3 initial phases had an in-scope document change recorded after an execution-tool turn with no direct edit or write, at call 29 OFF and call 12 ON. ## Bounded fresh-task pilot

**Question.** Can a single evidence-based advisory or deterministic correction improve owner-accepted tasks per wall hour at equal acceptance quality and equal or lower paid spend? Treat this as a feasibility screen. Eight pairs are too few to distinguish a small acceptance effect from run-to-run variation. A viable signal is at least two additional owner-accepted tasks out of eight, or at least 20% higher end-to-end accepted tasks per hour at the same or higher acceptance count, with paid cost per accepted task no higher and all quality controls passing.

1. Use up to eight fresh paired tasks, four constrained implementations with Q38FN OFF and four assertion-preservation tasks with A3B. Pick the tasks and deterministic controls before inspecting outcomes. Each pair uses the same brief, source, tools, route, context allowance, sampling and task and paid budgets. Give each arm a separate fresh session. Counterbalance arm ordering and record slot/cache and shared workload. Start with a two-pair shadow checkpoint, one per route, with both arms under the current driver. Replace neither slot cache nor session history with past copies. Collect configured, triggered, delivered and acted-on as distinct events. If a shadow advisory fires on a subsequently accepted trace, inspect the timestamp and artifact/check evidence before enabling delivery. Six pairs remain for live comparison after the checkpoint, and the shadow pairs stay separately reported.
2. Give both arms at most 3,600 seconds local on Q38FN or 1,800 seconds local on A3B, 120,000 charged generated tokens per task chain and a 32,768 per-request ceiling. The treatment reserves its last 15% of phase wall time, 540 or 270 seconds, and 16,384 chain tokens for at most one same-session correction. Make the first checkpoint at a safe turn boundary no later than the reserve boundary by bounding in-flight request time and reservations. If the current scoped patch has a deterministic, actionable failing check and at least 120 seconds and 4,096 charged tokens remain, issue one correction with the exact diagnostic, scope and preservation requirement. Otherwise use any remaining time to continue normal work or to verify the frozen candidate. Preserve execution tools. If a single advisory is delivered, ask for a concrete edit, focused check or exact blocker and carry on within the same deadline. Preserve negative and censored outcomes. No extra local phase or automatic fresh retry is part of the treatment. Record any bounded fallback as fresh with or without the frozen draft.
3. If necessary, permit one paid implementation fallback and up to two source reviews total per task and arm. Cap paid work cumulatively at 900 seconds, 300,000 total input tokens and 12,000 output tokens, including review. Set reservations against those totals before a paid call. Debit paid guidance, diagnosis, review, owner management and fallback usage once each. Report cached input, uncached input, output, billed cost where known and per-task management time. End-to-end wall runs from dispatch through acceptance or terminal closure and includes local inference, tools, grader, review, fallback and orchestration. Separately disclose shared setup and unmetered stages. Report exact accepted counts, per-route paired wall deltas, equal-quality accepted tasks per hour and paid cost per accepted task. Keep tasks with no acceptance in the wall/cost denominator and report local-only outcomes separately.
4. Read the outcomes after each two-pair block. Stop on an assertion/control violation, unauthorized diffs, lost receipts, an exceeded cap, or a treatment-specific rejection tied to a warning or correction. Stop for futility when even perfect remaining treatment results cannot reach the acceptance target and the measured wall/cost bounds rule out the speed path. Stop at eight pairs in all cases. For the six possible live pairs, require two extra accepted tasks or the same 20% speed/cost screen and report the exact denominators. Publish paired outcomes and uncertainty, including untouched accepted controls, missed stalls and accepted late edits. The pilot screens whether a larger matched test is justified. The original fixtures already have a historical main-only comparator.

## Reproduction

Run from the root checkout with the evidence commit available locally. For a byte-identical report, use the orchestrator checkout at `59a1d469516ccf56f459f0f8254d423eda1748c6`, recorded in the replay. The default reads the immutable Git archive. `--runtime` is an optional fast path for a mounted copy of the source receipts and verifies the same hashes.

```sh
python3 scripts/autopilot_replay/w66_recovery.py \
  --orchestrator /workspace/repos/epyc-orchestrator \
  --output /tmp/w66-recovery-replay.json
cmp /tmp/w66-recovery-replay.json artifacts/analysis/ap-w66-recovery/replay.json
```
