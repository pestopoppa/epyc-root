# V5I (matrix-core Q8) on A3B: why 0.988, and what would move it

Date: 2026-11-22. Source: existing data only, no compute.
Question: V5I wins on 27B (1.10x np1, 1.16x np2) but scored 0.988x on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MTP (MI210, MTP draft-n-max 2). Why?

## Evidence

Row `ak-v5i-a3b-mtp-e2e-gpu-20261121`, `/mnt/raid0/llm/tmp/ak-v5i-a3b-mtp-e2e-gpu-20261121/out/RESULT.json`, binary 1778cda76:

- The model is `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MTP-Q8_0.gguf`, all Q8_0 except the F32 routers.
- The recipe was np1, `-c 8192`, `-ctk f16`, MTP n_max 2, so verify runs n=3 rows.
- The verdict was `FUTILITY_BELOW_BASELINE`, after only **3 pairs** (3 requests of about 190 tokens each). It came from the 3-pair rule "mean r < 1.0". The KEEP/LOSS CI gate needs at least 4 pairs and was never reached.
- tps ratios were 0.989, 0.986 and 0.988 (CI 0.983 to 0.992).
- mean_len was 2.642 for V and 2.686 for X (ratio 0.984). Acceptance was 0.825 for V and 0.845 for X.
- **The implied per-round cost ratio V/X is 0.996.** The decomposition gives about 0.4% faster rounds and 1.6% shorter accepted runs.
- The X arm alone is not deterministic. Content hashes differ between X runs of the same prompt (pairs 0/1/2), and per-request mean_len moves by ±0.1 within an arm. The acceptance gap is about 1.5 SE and is not separable from DF2/MTP nondeterminism at n≈1700 drafts. Pair 0 has equal mean_len but tps 0.989, so per-round cost is somewhere between −0.4% and +1%.

## Cause

1. **There is almost nothing for V5I to accelerate.** On 27B every weight matmul is dense Q8_0, so V5I covers about all of decode. On A3B, V5I only takes the dense Q8_0 MUL_MATs: attention q/k/v/o, the GDN projections, the shared expert, and the MTP head. The routed experts go through MUL_MAT_ID, which at n≤8 uses MMVQ (and split16 at 9 to 16 rows) and never reaches V5I. The routers are F32 and use MMVF/MMF. The dense share of A3B step time is small, and at n=3 MMVQ on those small matrices is already close to its bandwidth point. The measured round-cost delta (≈0.4%) fits this "no coverage" picture. **Hypothesis (a) holds in its weak form**: the problem is coverage, not a per-launch penalty. There is no evidence of a loss per launch.
2. **The headline 0.988 is mostly acceptance noise plus an early stop.** The 3-pair futility rule fired on a 1.2% gap that sits within the arm's own nondeterminism. V5I's different accumulation order also feeds the MTP head (its matmuls are Q8_0 too), so draft argmaxes can flip near ties. That shifts acceptance in either direction. It does not mean V5I is slower.
3. Hypothesis (b), INPLACE repack corrupting another reader: **no evidence**. The oracle reported 0/72 top-1 disagreements in both arms, and correctness held. Repacked tensors are dense weights that only MUL_MAT reads.
4. Hypothesis (c), an MFMA MUL_MAT_ID kernel: **mostly rejected as framed.** At np1 to np4 (verify n=3 to 12), each step routes to n×8 expert slots across 256 experts, which is ≈0.5 to 1 token per touched expert. MFMA pays off only when the same weight tile is reused across many tokens. At ≤1 token per expert the op is bound by weight bytes, and bytes grow with the number of **unique experts touched**, which grows nearly linearly in n below saturation. The per-row cost therefore cannot go flat. `ak-mmid-split16-gpu-20261121-r4` `moe_step_ms` shows this: OFF rises about 1.1 ms/row for n=1 to 8 (1.81 to 9.68), and split16 rises 1.18 ms/row for n=9 to 16. That slope is the byte cost of new experts, not kernel overhead. The available headroom is bandwidth efficiency: down n1 runs at 639 GB/s and split16 at n10 at 695 GB/s, against about 1.6 TB/s HBM peak.

## For the MFMA MUL_MAT_ID build (added 2026-11-22, after the operator approved it)

- **Expert quant mix:** `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MTP-Q8_0.gguf` stores every expert tensor as Q8_0: 256 experts, 8 used, hidden 2048, expert FFN 512. The routers (`ffn_gate_inp` [2048,256]) are F32, and the MTP-layer router is BF16. The test-backend-ops shapes are in the split16 row: `type_a=q8_0, n_mats=256, n_used=8, m=512|2048, k=2048|512`.
- **Expert occupancy vs n**, assuming uniform routing: unique experts U(n) = 256·(1−(1−8/256)^n). This gives n=3 → 23 experts at 1.03 tok/expert, n=6 → 45 at 1.07, n=12 → 83 at 1.16, and n=16 → 101 at 1.27. Real routing is skewed, so occupancy runs a little higher than this. Verify n is 3 at np1 (MTP n_max 2) and up to about 12 at np4.
- **Expected per-row cost:** one expert across 40 layers is 3·2048·512·1.0625 B·40 ≈ 134 MB. Each added row brings about 7.5 new experts at small n, so about 1.0 GB per row. That puts the floor at ≈0.63 ms/row at 1.6 TB/s peak and ≈0.9 ms/row at a realistic 1.1 TB/s. This is a full-model e2e estimate. Measured slopes in `moe_step_ms` (op-case units) are about 1.1 ms/row for MMVQ at n≤8 and 1.18 ms/row for split16 at n 9 to 16. The kernel's job is to approach the byte floor. With ~1 tok/expert, MFMA tile reuse does not lower that floor, so the realistic target is ≤0.9 ms/row, about 1.2 to 1.3x on the MoE op. A flat per-row cost is not reachable until tokens per expert are well above 1, i.e. at large batch or prefill.
- **Acceptance test for the kernel:** compare with split16 ON at n=3..16 on the split16 row's op cases, and report achieved GB/s as well as us. The bar is ≥1000 GB/s at n≥4. At n=1 MMVQ already reaches 639 GB/s.
