## Verdict: dive-overturned for the framing "sentence-level alternative we could test"
SkipKV is a **chain-of-thought** method. It permanently evicts KV during decode of R1-distilled reasoning models and adds a steering vector to shorten the output. The sentence concept is a redundancy penalty, not a retention unit.

## Does it explain our Expected-Attention failure?
Shared observation: scattered token eviction drops digit/number tokens and the model then answers with a near-miss. That matches AM-PORT-2B (EA keep 0.8 = 1/32; failures like '6910' for 6906140; L4 at 0.5 stride 2 fails the same way; long-context-program.md:905-906 @ ac1347f). Differences that stop it being an explanation: (1) SkipKV's setting is generated CoT text re-read by the same model, ours is a prompt-resident needle; (2) its remedy is penalising duplicate sentences, a needle sentence is unique; (3) it never evaluates a prompt-retrieval task or a hybrid model. The more direct evidence for our failure is our own: L4 with stride 1 (contiguous truncation, 45/60 at 0.8) beats EA (1/32), i.e. contiguity of kept tokens matters, which is the property SnapKV pooling provides (see intake-2775).

## Sentence/chunk-level alternative to test
SkipKV supplies none. The testable arm (our design, motivated by its Obs 3 and by RocketKV's pooling): partition the prompt at delimiters (newline, sentence end, digit-run boundaries), score a span by the mean (or max) EA score of its tokens, and keep or drop whole spans to the budget; compare against pooled-EA (intake-2775 A1) and plain EA on the 60-item probe at keep 0.8/0.5.
