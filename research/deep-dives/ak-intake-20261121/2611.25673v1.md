Overall mean 56.7 reproduced from the abstract and §3.2. Table 12 CIs: the top-vs-bottom overall intervals do not overlap but adjacent ranks overlap heavily (e.g. 68.5 [62.0,74.5] vs 65.5 [59.0,72.0]); Workspace n=25 gives intervals of 40 pp width, so Workspace rankings are noise.
2. Protocol: tasks pass^3 on the fixed executor, >=8 rounds, one successful reference trajectory; boundaries 25/50/75% assigned one per trajectory after length stratification; compactor sees H_b with a target ratio r (default 10% characters) via IRC; executor gets task + M_b + fixed continuation instruction, resumes from boundary environment state, capped at T-b additional actions; each compacted context generated once and evaluated by 3 independent continuations. Executors: Claude Opus 4.8 (BrowseComp, SWE-bench), GPT-5.5 Codex (CompanyBench).
3. Task counts: 79 BrowseComp, 96 SWE-bench, 25 CompanyBench (=200). Mean trajectory tokens 35.3K / 8.8K / 122.8K. Note SWE-bench trajectories average only 8.8K tokens, so 'compaction' there is of a small context and the 10% target (~0.9K tokens) is a severe squeeze.
4. Alternative explanations tested (Table 18, G.4): prefix length is essentially uncorrelated with accuracy within a domain (|rho| <= 0.03); the four strongest compactors jointly fail 15.2% of Search, 7.3% of Coding, 32.0% of Workspace tasks, so failures are not only weak-compactor failures.

## Local design transfer
Yes, the protocol can be run on our served models: compactor = served model, executor = a fixed served model, tasks = public SWE-bench-style repositories already used in the harness, budget = T-b actions. Mapped against TOC-CMP-4 (context-and-token-economy.md:L76 @ ac1347f; sub-boxes L77-L90): TOC-CMP-4 already pre-registers MDE 0.10 blocked actions/step, >=30 paired points per model with 8-12 repeats, three arms and a boundary-stratified bootstrap, i.e. most of the statistical skeleton. What CompAct-Bench adds that TOC-CMP-4 lacks: matched action budget, ratio calibration, pass^3 pre-filter of uncompacted solvability, persistence-floor arm, 25/50/75 boundary placement, error annotation. Differences: TOC-CMP-4 varies the compaction POLICY with a fixed model; CompAct-Bench varies the compactor MODEL with fixed executor and policy.

## Compact-Bench (intake-2629, arXiv 2610.31710) comparison
Complementary, not redundant. Compact-Bench (index entry): paired full-history vs summary-only continuations from a shared repository state, 14 summarizer backbones, coding-only, measures 'stable damage' 5-64% of 22 full-history successes, and finds LLM summaries retain at most 16.0% of recurring details vs 79.1% for mechanical extraction. CompAct-Bench: 200 tasks across search/coding/workspace, 12 compactors, boundary-stage and retention-ratio control, error taxonomy and state-persistence analysis. Shared core idea: paired continuation from a restored state. Compact-Bench supplies the mechanical-vs-LLM contrast (directly our TOC arms); CompAct-Bench supplies the boundary/ratio/persistence controls. The two disagree in sign on one thing worth noting: Compact-Bench says coding summaries are heavily damaging (up to 64% of successes), CompAct-Bench's Coding is the easiest domain (66.7-86.1%) because environment state persists; different task construction (22 long coding tasks vs 96 short SWE-bench ones at 8.8K tokens).

## Adversarial pass
- Annotation of failure categories is LLM-generated with a manual spot review (G.2); no inter-annotator agreement is reported in the text I read. Treat Table 16/17 as descriptive.
- Single compaction prompt for all models (A.2) and fixed 10% retention: model rankings may reflect prompt fit and instruction following on length, not compaction ability; IRC mitigates length but not prompt fit.
- Executors are frontier closed models; local 27B executors will have different recovery behavior, so absolute Table 2 numbers do not transfer.
- Retention measured in characters, not tokens, so code-heavy contexts (token-dense) get a different effective token ratio.
- Independent replication: none found; paper is v1 two days old (19 Nov 2026).
- Cross-check against Slipstream (intake-2770): Slipstream attributes ~90% of failures to omission; CompAct finds critical omission in ~48% and premature commitment in 46-57% of failures. Different workloads and taxonomies (Slipstream's are labelled from real traces, 3 buckets); the discrepancy is real and means omission-dominance is not established.
