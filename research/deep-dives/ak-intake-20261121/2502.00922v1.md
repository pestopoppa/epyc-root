## What it actually measures

- **Everything is simulated except decoder synthesis.** The performance numbers come from a modified SCALE-Sim / STAR-Sim analytical model of a 128x128 16 nm systolic array (Table 2) and from Timeloop/Accelergy for a Simba/NVDLA-like vector accelerator (Table 3), at 64 and 128 GB/s DRAM. Only prefill-like single queries at benchmark-average input lengths (25-92 tokens) are modelled. The one hardware artefact is a Verilog 5-bit Huffman decoder plus a PE synthesised for GF12: PE 484 um2, decoder 1199.3 um2 at Lmax=12, so 6.13% overhead for a 128x128 array. **The abstract says "simulations and an FPGA prototype", but no FPGA result appears anywhere in the text.**
- **Weights are FP16/BF16 only, never quantized.** FP16 is split {1,5,5,5}: raw sign plus three 5-bit Huffman streams decoded by 32-entry CAMs, 10.61 bits entropy vs 10.54 for whole-word coding on Llama-3-8B. BF16 is split 1-4-4-7, where the 7 mantissa bits "show little to no compressibility". Table 5: FP16 compresses 1.46-1.47x (Llama/Qwen, 10.9-11.0 b/param) and 1.16-1.17x (OPT/Vicuna, 13.7 b). BF16 compresses 1.37-1.38x (11.6-11.7 b) for every model.
- Latency savings: 26-31% (Llama) and 13-15% (OPT). Energy savings: 16-26% and 3-10%. Both track the compression ratio, as expected from a bandwidth-bound analytical model. At 256 GB/s the baseline is already compute-bound and latency does not improve; energy still drops 24%.
- **Red flag:** Table 5 reports identical bits/param to two decimals across architecturally different models (Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3-8B, Qwen-2.5-3B and Qwen-2.5-7B all at 10.96 FP16 / 11.68 BF16; nearly every BF16 row at 11.68 or 11.59). That looks like an aggregation artefact. Table 1 gives Llama-3-8B FP16 entropy as 10.54 bits, while Table 5's 10.96 includes Huffman overhead.

## Does it support or contradict LazyWeights?

**It supports LazyWeights on every overlapping point and contradicts nothing.**
1. Lossless coding of 16-bit weights keeps about 70% of the size (11 b/param), which is the figure LazyWeights quotes for this lineage.
2. Both papers require a hardware decoder in the data path. Huff-LLM cites NeuZip as getting 33% compression with a 33% inference slowdown from software decompression, the same direction as LazyWeights' 2.5-3.2x software-decode penalty.
3. Both use small-alphabet split or pair symbols so that a single-cycle decoder is feasible: CAM for 5-bit symbols in Huff-LLM, compare-against-limits for an 11-bit-limited code in LazyWeights.

Huff-LLM is weaker evidence: simulated hypothetical accelerators, FP16/BF16 weights, and no quantized formats.
