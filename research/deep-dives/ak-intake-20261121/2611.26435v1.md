## Verified facts (the three that matter)
1. P99/throughput headline = simulator. Quote: "evaluate its P99 latency and throughput with a simulator calibrated against the same hardware. We use simulations to limit the cost of the many runs needed." Accuracy only is live. Simulator validated on vanilla only: Table 4, within 3.4% at lambda=0.2, but median/mean off by up to 16% at lambda=0.05 (host speed varied 21%). Live Aggron code exists ("Live runs load Aggron into unmodified vLLM 0.24 as a subclass of its scheduler, and the simulator runs the same controller code") but no live P99/throughput table is reported.
2. Baselines: vanilla (no compaction, 131K window), Slipstream and APEC "compact the whole context at 60K". The 27-31% is vs those. Against a synchronous 4K prose cap the text says throughput is the same ("A synchronous 4K prose cap reaches the same throughput on these traces"). Queue ablation (Fig 9a): separate vs shared queue +1.9-8.0% throughput, -3.8-8.1% P99 on Qwen; +1.1-1.4% / -1.2-1.4% on GLM; +0.1-0.8% / -0.4-0.5% on Gemma. Removing the hideability gate costs up to 3.8% throughput, +47% P99 (light load).
3. Gains at frontier scale (Fig 10, simulated, 4 frontier models, TP 8-32 H100): +5-14% throughput under a 120K cap, +3-14% under 24K; DSV4 (3.4 KiB KV/token) LOSES 1.7%. Gain scales with KV bytes per token, so models with compact KV (hybrid/GDN such as our Qwen3.8/3.5 family, or MLA) benefit least. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B in Section 6.1 has 3/4 linear-attention layers, yet shows the largest gain because its programs fit the window so vanilla never compacts - an arrangement the paper itself says inflates Section 6.1 gains relative to 6.4.

## Does the idea need multi-request queueing?
The queue-time trigger and separate summarization queue: yes, they only function when a request waits for admission (when no queue to hide behind "the server reverts to synchronous summarization"). The prose-only selective compression, frozen append-only summaries and small caps: no, they are independent (Table 1/Observation 2 are single-agent accuracy measurements; Table 2 shows the 4K cap helps even at lambda=0.01 in HBM use 1.57x). Our stack: llama.cpp slots with n_parallel 1-2 per server plus the orchestrator-side SharedKVPoolAdmission (kv-cache-program.md KPF-22 line 313) - a queue exists, but head-of-line blocked share has not been measured (A3). llama-server `--cache-ram`/slot-save paths exist in kv-cache-program.md (line 224 slot save/restore GB/s measurements); no existing task treats a stalled request's idle time as compaction capacity.

## Adversarial pass
Independent replication: none found in the text; the paper is a single-group v1. Self-acknowledged limits: trajectory replay assumes summarization leaves trajectories within run variation (22 of 24 arm pairs pass; SWE grow-1k on Qwen and GLM do not, with 1.3-1.7x more tool calls after summaries). Reviewer-style objections: (1) headline from simulator calibrated on one H100 and 'a few constants'; (2) baselines at 60K are a straw regime since the paper's own Table 1 shows async at 4K would also win on queue but loses accuracy; (3) SWE-bench n=100 stratified, Terminal-Bench 30-40 scored, noise +-12-20 pp; (4) Table 1 shows SWE accuracy drops up to 6 pp when whole-context (incl. code) is summarized at 4K - the very result motivating prose-only - on 100 tasks, itself within noise. Contradiction with Slipstream (intake-2770): Aggron states its results 'run counter to prior findings that asynchronous summarization outperforms synchronous'; Slipstream measured single-agent latency without KV saturation, Aggron under load.
