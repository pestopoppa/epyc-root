## Key claims (corrected)
0. ChunkKV is SnapKV-style without pooling: sum the last-w query attention per key, sum within non-overlapping fixed chunks of c tokens (default c=10), keep top floor((Lmax-w)/c) whole chunks plus the last w tokens, same index set per layer group via layer-wise reuse (Alg 1, Alg 3, Alg 2).
1. At matched KV budget it beats SnapKV/PyramidKV mostly on small budgets: NIAH LLaMA-3.1-8B at 128 entries 73.8% vs SnapKV 58.9 / PyramidKV 65.1 (full 74.6); LongBench at 10% LLaMA-3-8B -2.29% vs SnapKV -3.16%. LongBench margins are within ~1 point and the paper does not state whether its SnapKV baseline used pooling.
2. Chunk size is a plateau, not a knob: LongBench 40.49/40.47/40.51/40.05/39.57 for c=3/5/10/20/30 (SnapKV 40.15); NIAH 65.6/69.1/73.8/72.0/71.2 (SnapKV 58.9). c=30 already loses to SnapKV on LongBench but not on NIAH.
3. Layer-wise index reuse (reuse=2) cuts compression cost with -0.29% to -0.59% LongBench; the +26.5% throughput is vs FullKV on one A40, batch 1, LLaMA-3-8B, and mixes the eviction benefit with the reuse benefit (ChunkKV without reuse is already +24.4%).

## Reader should conclude
Cite ChunkKV for: the chunk-sum + top-k + keep-window algorithm, the c=5-20 plateau with c=10 default, and that fixed-size chunk eviction beat SnapKV/PyramidKV at tiny NIAH budgets on LLaMA-3/Mistral. Do NOT cite it as showing span-granular eviction fixes a key-only proxy (EA), as evidence on hybrid/GDN models, or as an isolated token-vs-span effect (no pooled-SnapKV control; third-party kvpress runs (search snippets, unreviewed) show chunk-size and grid-offset sensitivity and one dialogue benchmark where ChunkKV trails SnapKV). The 26.5% throughput is a one-GPU batch-1 vs FullKV number.
