## Verified facts
- Table 1 (accuracy, Sync vs Slipstream): Qwen3.5-9B SWE 4k 23.4->29.8 (+6.4), 6k 22.0->30.8 (+8.8), 8k 20.4->29.2 (+8.8); BrowseComp 32k 53.3->57.3 (+4.0), 48k 52.7->57.3 (+4.6), 64k 55.3->58.0 (+2.7). Seed-OSS-36B SWE 29.6->35.8 (+6.2), 30.6->37.4 (+6.6), 38.0->40.6 (+2.6); BrowseComp 46.7->48.7 (+2.0), 52.0->53.3 (+1.3), 49.3->52.7 (+3.4). All 12 cells positive. The paper gives no task count, seeds or intervals in the text I read; BrowseComp gains of 1.3-2.0 pp are within plausible sampling noise.
- Latency: -11.3% to -39.7% (Fig 4b), larger at smaller thresholds; compaction is 26-44% of Sync latency; judge/update overhead <1%.
- Hardware: vLLM 0.19.1; Qwen3.5-9B on one H100 80GB, Seed-OSS-36B on two.
- Mechanism details: default threshold ~20% of average full trajectory length plus 2/3x and 4/3x; judge accept if score >= 7/10; on reject, brief pause and targeted summary repair, else fall back to sync; next-k is whatever overlapped (avg 2.1 steps BrowseComp, 3.7 SWE); error locality: BrowseComp 97% at k=1, 100% by k=2; SWE 60% at k=1, 88% by k=3, 96% by k=6.

## Relation to Aggron and CompAct-Bench
- Aggron (intake-2761) treats Slipstream as a baseline (compaction at 60K, whole-context, async) and reports that async gives little latency gain under KV saturation and that frequent small prose-only summaries beat it in its simulator; Slipstream measured sequential per-query latency without a saturated KV pool. The two address different regimes and should not be merged.
- CompAct-Bench (intake-2762) measures the same failure (compaction-induced loss) offline with a fixed executor; Slipstream is an online mitigation using the executor's own next steps as the oracle. CompAct-Bench's 'counterfactual continuation' is Slipstream's 'next-k on the original context'.
- Omission dominance: Slipstream ~90% of failures omission; CompAct finds six overlapping categories with critical omission only ~48% of failures. Not reconcilable from the texts.

## Consistency flag
SWE-bench gains of +8.8 pp (Qwen3.5-9B at 6k and 8k) with a reported judge rejection rate of 5.4-8.5%: for the accuracy gain to come only from repaired rejected summaries, nearly every rejected case would have to flip to success. Possible other contributors: the next-k steps appended on acceptance, or noise. The Async-only ablation (accuracy close to Sync) rules out asynchrony as the source but does not isolate what in the judge path contributes. Treat the size of the gain as unexplained.
