## Settles
new entry. Settles the exact scoring rule: sum over window queries of per-head softmax attention, 1D stride-1 pool (max in the paper, avg indistinguishable), per-head top-k, window concatenated. The proposed EA-POOL screen reuses the pooling step but NOT the signal: SnapKV pools real observed attention, our EA is a key-only proxy against a mean key (src/llama-kv-compress.cpp score_layer), so a SnapKV result does not transfer by citation.

## Key claims (corrected)
0. SnapKV scores prompt positions by summing softmax attention from the last L_obs (observation window) queries onto the prefix keys, then applies a stride-1 1D pooling (padding kernel//2) over that vote vector BEFORE per-head top-k, and always keeps the window itself (Listing 1).
1. Pooling is the only ablated piece: a LongEval-Lines retrieval ablation on Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 (kernel 5, window 16) says pooling "significantly" improves retrieval; the numbers are in an image (Fig 8) and the paper states max vs average pooling made no significant difference. No kernel-size sweep appears in the text (kernel 5, 7, 13 are used in different experiments).
2. Stated mechanism for pooling is copy-completeness: attention weights the initial portion of a critical cluster highest and models copy the neighbouring tokens (induction heads), so a bare top-k keeps fragments (the phone-number example); this is a hypothesis, not tested by an intervention.
3. Headline LongBench quality (kernel 7, window 32; 1024/2048/4096 prompt budget vs H2O at 4096) and Command-R RAG (-1.2% / -2.1%) are attention-only models at ~13k mean prompt length, 68-92% prompt compression, prompt-phase only; nothing on hybrid/GDN models.

## Adversarial pass
Max vs average: the paper's own statement only; the NeurIPS version reportedly adds a kernel-1 (no pooling) configuration (search snippet, not verified here). No independent critique of kernel size found; a community MLX reimplementation notes the max-pool step is not implemented there (search snippet), a reminder that "SnapKV" numbers in other papers may be unpooled. ChunkKV's own baseline does not say whether it pools (its text has no 'pool'), so ChunkKV-vs-SnapKV gaps may partly be a pooling gap. Reviewer-style objection: the retrieval ablation is one 7B model on a synthetic key-value task with the result in a figure; "no difference max vs avg" has no numbers. Restatement: the Command-R and RAG results are the authors' (Cohere-internal benchmarks) and not independently reproduced.

## Reader should conclude
Cite SnapKV for: the exact pooling rule (stride-1 1D max-pool over summed window attention before top-k, kernel 5/7/13, window 16/32/64, window kept) and that max vs avg did not matter. Do NOT cite it as evidence that pooling rescues a key-only/position-independent scorer such as our EA, nor for hybrid/GDN models, nor for any kernel size above 13 (63 is RocketKV, untested here). The ablation numbers are in an image; do not quote a percentage.
