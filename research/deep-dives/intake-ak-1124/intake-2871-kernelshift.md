## 2. Key claims
0. **Repo-to-benchmark pipeline is agent-built with deterministic scripts and human gates.** CONFIRMED. (anchors in drafts/kernelshift.json.) Four "construction skills" (gen-definition, gen-workload, gen-candidate-solution, gen-baseline-solution) are driven by Claude Code, with `kgen` scripts for the mechanical steps. Humans pick kernels, approve workloads that cannot be traced from a model run, and review each expert wrapper after its first evaluation. A kernel is dropped if the expert is missing, fails correctness, or is under 2x faster than the compiler-auto-vectorised scalar starting point.
1. **"Agents beat auto-vectorization 2.4-19.8x."** PARTIAL. (anchors in drafts/kernelshift.json.) Reference point: the starting point is portable scalar C++ compiled with `-O3` and SVE enabled, on Graviton3, over the 79 SVE kernels. The 2.4-19.8x range is each system's per-source geomean over that starting point (Table 16), not a win over a tuned autovectoriser. Experts are 2.1-28.2x over the same starting point. The abstract's "everywhere" means every system in every source column.
2. **"Up to 2.74x over ncnn Neon, 0.70x of KleidiAI."** PARTIAL. (anchors in drafts/kernelshift.json.) 2.743 is the single best cell (nanobot + GLM-5.3, geomean of 23 ncnn kernels, 40 tool calls). The best vs-ncnn cell has a bootstrap 95% CI of [2.05, 3.68]. 0.701 is Claude Code + Opus 4.8 on only 6 KleidiAI SVE kernels (CI [0.45, 0.98]); it beats the reference on 2 of the 6 and loses on 4. The llama.cpp column is not in the steering text: best 1.954 (GLM-5.3), 85% beat-ref.
3. **"Agent kernels give 1.3-1.6x prefill over llama.cpp's fast path in two LLMs."** PARTIAL. (anchors in drafts/kernelshift.json.) What the paper actually did:
   - Hardware: Graviton4 (Neoverse V2), 1/4/16 threads.
   - Models: Qwen3.5-4B (11 GEMM shapes) and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (7 GEMM shapes), both Q4_K_M. The shapes include q4_K, q5_K and q6_K weights.
   - Baseline ("fast path"): stock llama.cpp using its **repacked i8mm** path. Original llama.cpp without repack is 0.46-0.52x prefill.
   - Harness: Claude Code with Fable 5.1, not the Table 2 agents.
   - Results: prefill 1.298-1.568, decode 1.045-1.102. Perplexity is unchanged (9.504 vs 9.506; 7.879 vs 7.879).
   - Each kernel is specialised to one fixed (N, K), and all 18 use i8mm with shape-specific tiling, layout and prefetch. Isolated kernels run 1.45x (Qwen) and 1.62x (Llama) over llama.cpp's repack.
   - Caveats in the paper's own table (Table 19, 16 threads): Llama-3.1-8B at micro-batch 64 is 0.989 (a loss), at 2 it is 1.598, at 8 it is 1.479; Qwen3.5-4B is 1.270 at 2, 1.164 at 8 and 1.061 at 64. The gain falls with prompt length (8192: 1.226 and 1.184). The authors state this is not a general claim that agents out-engineer expert libraries.
4. **"Agents fail to adapt to SME2 matrix tiles."** CONFIRMED with a model dependence. (anchors in drafts/kernelshift.json.) On Apple M4, none of the final kernels in the SME2 ladder tier use ZA outer products (the agent wrote ZA code in only 2 of 60 runs, neither passing). On the 6 KleidiAI kernels in three seeds, final solutions using ZA are 15/18 (Opus 4.8), 5/18 (Sonnet 4.6), 5/18 (GLM-5.3) and 1/18 (DeepSeek-V4-Pro). Claude models show ratio ~1 SVE-to-SME2; DeepSeek, GLM and GPT-5.6-luna fall to 0.41-0.46. Opus 4.8 reached 0.94x the expert on gemm_bf16_n512_k512 after 29 calls, and the trajectory is in Appendix E.2. Adding pitfalls to the prompt doubled the 20-kernel SME2 score (0.083 to 0.167), but 5 of 20 kernels got worse.
5. **Arm-only scope; single core.** CONFIRMED. (anchors in drafts/kernelshift.json.) The paper says x86 and GPU transfer is untested and the kernel evaluation is single-core with multithreading rejected.

## 3. Reported results (as stated)
- **Table 2** (Graviton3, SVE, 40 tool calls, geomean Speedup@40 vs expert; columns ncnn/llama.cpp/SIMD Loops/KleidiAI): best ncnn 2.743 (nanobot+GLM-5.3); best llama.cpp 1.954 (same); best SIMD Loops 1.555 (Claude Code+Opus 4.8); best KleidiAI 0.701 (Claude Code+Opus 4.8). Single-shot with no tools (Opus 4.8): 0.577 / 0.324 / 0.852 / 0.152. Scalar -O3 SVE-enabled start: 0.229 / 0.126 / 0.468 / 0.035.
- **Table 3 ladder**: Graviton4 32-kernel subset: Neon-only 1.105, +SVE 0.953, +SVE2 1.101 (nanobot+GPT-5.6-luna, 3 seeds). Apple M4, 20 KleidiAI SME2 kernels: Neon-only 0.141, +streaming SVE 0.059, +ZA/FMOPA 0.085.
- **Table 5 ablations** (GPT-5.6-luna): control 1.074; +Arm docs 0.997; +cross-kernel notepad 0.974; no disassemble tool 0.999; **no intrinsics 0.300**. SME2: control 0.083, +docs 0.130, +pitfalls 0.167.
- **Fig/Spearman**: median agent Speedup@40 vs expert strength over scalar: rho = -0.69 (all 79) and -0.75 (llama.cpp). llama.cpp RMSNorm: expert is 1.3x scalar and agents beat it by a median 6.0x. q4_K_M GEMM and MoE: expert is 25-29x scalar and agents reach 0.57-0.69x.
- **Table 17 (fraction of theoretical limit, Graviton3 single core)**: ncnn and llama.cpp experts reach about 7-8%, frontier agents 14-17%, KleidiAI expert 43% (best agent 30%).
- **Test-time scaling**: most gain in the first 40 calls; Claude Sonnet 4.6 is 23-25% higher at 100 calls.
- **Measurement protocol**: 10 warmup + 50 samples, min sample, each sample at least 10 ms, pinned to one CPU, fresh subprocess per evaluate, 1200 s cap. Re-measuring fixed code varies 0.2%; the agent's run-to-run spread is 4.5% (32-kernel geomean) and a median 9.7% per kernel.

## 4. Performance-principles table
| Principle (paper) | MI210 gfx90a | Zen 5 EPYC 9655 |
|---|---|---|

The paper gives no GPU data, so agent MFMA competence is unmeasured. A benchmark of MFMA tasks is the experiment. | NO direct analogue. AVX-512 has no tile-accumulator register file. Zen 5 has no AMX (uncited, general knowledge) although `ggml/src/ggml-cpu/amx/` exists in the tree. The closest idiom is register-blocked `vpdpbusd` / `vdpbf16ps` accumulators (see section 5). The agent's SME2 failure was about streaming-mode state, predicates and gathers, which has no x86 equivalent, so the failure does not predict VNNI behaviour. |
| Incremental extensions are adopted readily (SVE to SVE2: ratios 0.94-1.05; SVE2-only svcdot/svcmla/svmatch used like the expert in 13/14 cases) | PARTIAL. Inference only: wave64 intrinsics and `v_dot4`-class ops are incremental. | YES (inference, not measured). VNNI, BF16 and VBMI are incremental dot-product and permute extensions; the paper's SVE2 result is the nearest evidence, and 3 of 4 models used SVE2-only ops on only 38-100% of kernels. |

## 5. In-house state (C21 `98ff8a4`, lane `f74b175`)
- **x86 idiom coverage already exists.** `_mm512_dpbusd_epi32` at `ggml/src/ggml-cpu/arch/x86/repack.cpp:125` and the 256-bit variants at L153-155 @ 98ff8a4. `_mm512_dpbf16_ps` BF16 GEMM at `ggml/src/ggml-cpu/iqk/iqk_gemm_floats.cpp:357-364` and L385-387 @ 98ff8a4. `_mm512_permutex2var_epi8` / `permutexvar_epi8` at `ggml/src/ggml-cpu/exl3.cpp:421-433` @ 98ff8a4. These are idioms an AK planner can imitate rather than discover.
- **Overlap with the benchmark's llama.cpp tasks.** The paper's 26 llama.cpp tasks (GEMM BF16/q8_0/q4_K_M, MoE, MHA/GQA/MLA, RMSNorm) map to our ops MUL_MAT, MUL_MAT_ID, FLASH_ATTN and RMS_NORM. The overlap is at the operator level only. The paper's expert reference is ggml's **Arm** CPU path: `arch/arm/repack.cpp:4185` uses `vmmlaq_s32` (i8mm) @ 98ff8a4. Ours is x86: `llamafile/sgemm.cpp` (tinyBLAS), `iqk/iqk_gemm_kquants.cpp` (q4_K), `iqk_gemm_legacy_quants.cpp` (q8_0), `iqk_gemm_floats.cpp` (BF16), `iqk_flash_attn.cpp` and `arch/x86/repack.cpp`. `ggml/src/ggml-cpu/kleidiai/` is in the tree but Arm-only. **No file or reference kernel is shared.** Models differ too: the paper uses Qwen1.5-MoE, OLMoE, DeepSeek-V3, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B and Q4_K_M weights. We serve Q8_0 and IQ4_XS-class quants (IQ quants are absent from the paper).
- **How the AK loop gives hardware feedback today** (`loop/program.md` L24-45): the planner may probe freely with llama-bench, `test-backend-ops -o OP --perf`, rocprofv3 and objdump; a build is followed by affected-op units, an independent reference, then alternating A/B with n >= 5. CPU references exist (`cpu_quant_reference.py` decodes stored quantised bytes and computes scalar dot products; `cpu_norm_reference.py`, `cpu_fusion_reference.py`, `cpu_fa_reference.py`; a CPU_REPACK oracle on real served experts). The loop's keep screens run at the quarter-24t shape. I did not verify whether CPU hardware performance counters (cycles/instructions/cache-misses) are fed to the CPU planner; the paper feeds them via evaluate where Linux perf is available.
- **Lane rows**: `handoffs/active/autokernel-loop.md` L8530-8620 (AK-CPU-AA-CALIB, AK-CPU-A3B-MMID-GATEUP-BW, AK-CPU-A3B-SHEXP-VERIFY-GAP, AK-CPU-A3B-SHEXP-SMALLN-AB, AK-CPU-A3B-SHEXP-Q0RM4-AB, AK-CPU-QUARTER-READCEIL measured 112.9 GB/s, AK-CPU-CEIL-MEASURED, AK-CPU-QUARTER-CEIL-ALL, AK-CPU-GDN-STATE-FWD, AK-CPU-C22-LEVERS) @ f74b175. The small-N verify-width shared-expert loss tracked there is the nearest in-house counterpart to the paper's micro-batch 2-8 gains.
- **Served path**: the paper's technique (agent-written, shape-specialised GEMM spliced via a hook) is not in the served path. The in-house route is a source-level keep on a `llama.cpp-experimental` branch, gated by the loop.

## 8. Contradiction / adversarial pass
- Reference strength drives the headline: the paper's own Spearman is -0.69 and it says large speedups often reflect under-optimised reference paths (see claim anchors). The abstract's 2.4-19.8x is over scalar -O3 code, not over expert code.
- KleidiAI SVE n = 6, CIs wide: 0.70 has CI [0.45, 0.98]; "0.70x" should not be read as a point estimate.
- Agent stochasticity: 4.5% on the 32-kernel geomean, 9.7% median per kernel; rows within 3-5% are tied; in paired comparisons with the best system 2, 5, 8 and 0 of 13 are not separable on the four columns.
- SME2 evidence rests on 20 kernels and one base model for the ladder (GPT-5.6-luna, weakest model); Opus 4.8 reaches 0.94x on one kernel. Streaming-SVE tier is worse than Neon only because agents abandon Neon.
- Contamination: expert code is public; the paper argues it is not reproduced (6 post-cutoff vs 14 older SME2 kernels score 0.088 vs 0.082).
- Single core, wall-clock; multi-core effects are excluded. Our CPU lane is multi-thread and bandwidth-bound, so a single-core microbench win may not move decode TPOT.
- No independent replication found; restatements only (project page).
