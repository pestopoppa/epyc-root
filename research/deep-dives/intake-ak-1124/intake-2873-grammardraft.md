## 1. Stage-1 fields

- **Title**: Grammar-as-a-Draft: More Tokens per Speculative Decoding Step on Grammar-Constrained Structured Outputs
- **Authors**: John Zhang, Silky Arora (Google)
- **Version**: arXiv 2611.28349v1, 20 Nov 2026. Implementation: vllm-torchtpu PR #3321, **open and unmerged**, head `4082e8a42e40301a1376cba0cafd7b2cc468a580` (+1406/−9, 13 files; read: file list and the rejection-sampler hunk only). The Rust runtime is internal and not released.
- **Categories**: speculative_decoding, inference_serving, tool_implementation
- **Novelty**: medium. Forced-token jump-forward is known (SGLang compressed FSM, intake-2402; llguidance fast-forward tokens, intake-2401; SGLang PR #17062, intake-2422), and our own SPEC-GRAM.5 box already proposed "forced-span-as-draft (verified, then forced-accept)". New here: (a) the merge and cut rule against a model drafter's proposal in ONE row, (b) a row wider than the drafter's share (K=8 vs K_M=3 for MTP), (c) skipping the LM head for GRAMMAR slots, (d) a measured account of tokenization drift, and (e) a decomposition of the speedup into tokens, τ and per-step factors (Table 2).
- **Relevance**: medium. The topic has an active in-house line (SPEC-GRAM.* in `speculative-decoding.md`), but our measured forced share on production tool-call traffic is small (8.34% on the XML path, 2.07-3.84% on JSON), and SPEC-GRAM.5 was declined on its 1.02-1.09x ceiling. The json_schema request class has never been measured from real outputs. That class looks like the paper's workload: fixed keys, enums, numbers. This is the open gap.
- **credibility_score**: 2. Not peer-reviewed (0); ≤12 months (+1); major lab, Google (+1); no commercial-bias deduction (no product claim); independent corroboration 0. The Rust runtime is the same authors' second implementation, not an independent reproduction, and intake-2422 is a different mechanism (extend-mode jump-forward, not draft rows). 0+1+1 = 2.
- **Verdict**: **worth_investigating**. The mechanism is sound and cheap to plumb into llama-server's chain path. Under our sample-and-compare MTP verify, the lossless version is "append forced tokens to `spec_draft`". But the paper's speedups come from a workload whose output is mostly fixed text. A share measurement on our json_schema classes must come first (ledger row A1). Do NOT read the paper's 2.7x as a transfer estimate.

## 2. Key claims

0. **Merge/cut rule (CONFIRMED; no tree handling, NOT-FOUND-IN-SOURCE).** The planner walks positions from the committed grammar state and handles four cases (§3.3). (1) A model token equal to the grammar's forced token is kept and flagged GRAMMAR. (2) At a free position, a grammar-allowed model token is kept as MODEL. (3) At the first model token that differs from the forced token, or that the grammar disallows, the planner stops taking model tokens. No later model token is reused, because it continues a tokenization the row does not take. (4) Forced tokens are then appended from the state reached, until the grammar forces nothing or the row has K slots. The grammar's own draft is the canonical encoding of the jump-forward string, kept only if the grammar accepts every token of it (§3.2). The verify step accepts GRAMMAR slots with no comparison. The first rejected slot still rejects every later slot, so a GRAMMAR slot after a rejected MODEL slot is discarded. The LM head runs only for MODEL slots and the bonus slot (§3.1). The paper defines rows as single chains; tree or multi-branch drafts appear nowhere. The PR's rejection sampler ORs `draft_flags == 2` into the accept mask in both its greedy and random samplers (PR head 4082e8a, `rejection_sampler.py` hunk, reading material).
   Anchors: page §Abstract "proposed tokens that equal the forced token are kept and flagged GRAMMAR , the proposal is cut where it leaves the forced path, and forced tokens fill the rest of a row"; §3.1 "The first rejected slot still rejects every slot after it"; §3.1 "its target logits are never read: the LM head runs only for MODEL slots and the bonus slot"; §3.3 "At the first model token that differs from the grammar’s token, or that the grammar does not allow, the planner stops taking model tokens".
1. **Acceptance length (CONFIRMED at B=1, with qualifiers).** τ goes from 2.06 to 5.43 (MTP, Qwen3.5-0.8B), 1.99 to 5.31 (MTP, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B), 1.57 to 4.13 (DFlash, Qwen3-4B) and 1.30 to 3.64 (n-gram, Qwen3-4B). It stays at 5.26-5.43 / 4.11-4.13 / 3.61-3.64 at B=1-64. The arms do not have the same width: GaaD uses K=8 with the drafter's K_M (3 for MTP, 7 for DFlash and n-gram), while the drafter alone uses K=K_M. The paper does NOT report what fraction of output tokens are GRAMMAR-forced (NOT-FOUND-IN-SOURCE). It does state that the benchmark favours GaaD (eleven fixed keys, fixed indentation and delimiters).
   Anchors: Abstract "from 2.06 to 5.43 for MTP on Qwen3.5-0.8B (1.99 to 5.31 on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B)"; Abstract "from 1.57 to 4.13 for DFlash and from 1.30 to 3.64 for n-gram on Qwen3-4B"; §7 "The benchmark favours GaaD: the grammar fixes most of each output (eleven keys, indentation and delimiters)".
2. **Speedup 2.66-2.81x at B=1, 1.14-1.70x at B=64 (PARTIAL, narrowed).** The numbers match Table 1. Table 2, however, splits each speedup into a tokens factor of 1.31-1.38, a τ factor of 2.62-2.81 and a per-step factor of 0.31-0.80. The tokens factor exists because vllm-torchtpu's drafter-alone greedy baseline picks a finer, non-canonical tokenization at a bf16 near-tie, and so emits 24-28% more tokens. **Dive arithmetic on Table 2 (τ × per-step, i.e. against a baseline that already tokenizes canonically):** at B=1 that leaves 1.93x (MTP 0.8B), 2.13x (MTP 35B-A3B), 2.02x (DFlash) and 2.08x (n-gram). At B=64 it leaves **0.82x, 0.93x, 0.97x and 1.28x**, so three of the four vllm-torchtpu configurations would be slower than the drafter alone. In the Rust runtime both arms tokenize canonically; there GaaD is 2.02-2.23x at concurrency 1, 1.37x at 64 and 1.22x at 128 (0.8B). The batch-64 gain is therefore engine-dependent, and in vLLM it rests on the tokenization artefact.
   Anchors: Abstract "decodes 2.66–2.81 × \times faster than the best configuration of each drafter alone at batch size 1 and 1.14–1.70 × \times faster at batch size 64"; §5.2 "The first comes from tokenization (Section 3.5 ) and is 1.31–1.38 at every point"; Table 2 "MTP Qwen3.5-0.8B 64 sync 1.38 2.64 0.31 1.14 × \times"; §5.2 "In the Rust runtime, where both arms tokenize canonically and emit about as many tokens, MTP with GaaD is still 2.02–2.23 × \times faster than MTP alone at concurrency 1".
3. **Mixed traffic: plain-text TPOT +7-42% (CONFIRMED, attribution narrowed).** In vllm-torchtpu (Qwen3.5-0.8B, MTP, per-batch synchronization rule), plain-request TPOT rises 7% / 36% at B=16 and 21% / 42% at B=64, with 25% / 50% of requests structured. Structured requests finish 2.2-3.2x sooner, and the whole workload 1.03-1.23x sooner. In the Rust runtime the rise is a further 1.07-1.47x. Part of the cost is not GaaD's: with MTP alone, structured requests already raise plain TPOT from 2.08 to 3.95-4.72 ms at B=16. The mechanism is that grammar-draft steps must be synchronous on an asynchronously scheduled engine (§3.4).
   Anchors: §5.6 "their TPOT rises by 7% and 36% at B = 16 B{=}16 and by 21% and 42% at B = 64 B{=}64"; §5.6 "with MTP alone, structured requests already raise the plain TPOT from 2.08 to 3.95–4.72 ms at B = 16 B{=}16".
4. **Not lossless (CONFIRMED; sampling not evaluated).** A GRAMMAR slot is accepted with probability 1. The output distribution at and after such slots therefore differs from grammar-masked decoding of the target: under sampling directly, and under greedy through tokenization drift. Greedy outputs in vllm-torchtpu: 0/64 token-identical to the drafter alone, with 24-28% fewer tokens. In the Rust runtime: 128/128 identical at concurrency 1 on 0.8B and 82/128 on 397B. All outputs match the regex. The two arms agree on 85-96% of enum fields, 47-69% of numbers and 50-74% of free-text fields. The fp32 rescoring reports "neither systematically more likely" (31/64 favour GaaD; mean log-likelihood −178.58 vs −178.51 on 0.8B, −137.51 vs −135.03 on 4B). Task accuracy and sampling are not measured. The authors call a sampling-correct acceptance rule "an open question".
   Anchors: Abstract "Accepting a GRAMMAR draft with probability 1 gives up the lossless guarantee of standard speculative decoding"; §7 "We do not evaluate sampling."; §5.4 "GaaD matches none of the 64 requests, at either batch size, and emits 24–28% fewer tokens"; §8 "With sampling, an acceptance rule that accounts for every tokenization of the forced text is an open question.".
5. **Platform and implementation (CONFIRMED).** One TPU v7x slice of four chips (eight TensorCores). Two engines: vllm-torchtpu on vLLM 0.33, and an internal Rust runtime that runs AOT StableHLO through PJRT. The change is scheduler-level, with no new kernel. Host planning costs 0.136 / 0.91 / 2.74 ms per engine step at B=1/16/64, which is 1.0-1.3% of the step.
   Anchors: §5.1 "One TPU v7x slice of four chips (eight TensorCores, topology 2x2x1) for both engines"; §1 footnote "The Rust runtime is internal and not publicly released at the time of writing"; §5.7 "0.136 ms per engine step at B = 1 B{=}1".

## 3. Reported results (as stated)

- Hardware: one TPU v7x slice of 4 chips (2x2x1). TP=1 for 0.8B and 4B, TP=4 for 35B-A3B, TP=8 for 397B-A17B-FP8.
- Workload: the JSON character-profile benchmark (SGLang compressed-FSM blog). 50 prompts cycled to 256 / 64 / 128 requests; a regex for a JSON object with eleven fixed keys (one nested) and fixed indentation; ten value fields (4 enum, 1 number, 5 free text); greedy; ≤256 output tokens; prefix caching off.
- Table 1 (vllm-torchtpu, 256 requests), τ alone → GaaD and best-vs-best speedup:
  - MTP 0.8B: 2.06→5.43, 2.68x (B1); 1.85x (B4); 1.59x (B16); 2.05→5.41, 1.14x (B64).
  - MTP 35B-A3B: 1.99→5.31, 2.81x (B1); 1.44x (B16); 1.21x (B64).
  - DFlash 4B: 1.57→4.13, 2.66x (B1); 1.95x (B4); 1.60x (B16); 1.27x (B64).
  - n-gram 4B: 1.30→3.64, 2.75x (B1); 2.09x (B16); 1.70x (B64).
- Ablations: MTP alone at K=8 runs 0.81-0.95x as fast as at K=3 (0.8B) and 0.67-0.89x (35B-A3B). GaaD with K=K_M is 1.03-2.52x faster than the best drafter alone.
- Table 2 factors: tokens 1.31-1.38; τ 2.62-2.81; per-step 0.73-0.80 at B1, falling to 0.31-0.46 at B64.
- Table 3 (Rust runtime, MTP+GaaD vs MTP alone): 0.8B 2.23x / 1.68x / 1.37x / 1.22x at concurrency 1/16/64/128; 397B-A17B-FP8 2.02x / 1.86x / 1.69x at concurrency 1/8/32.
- Table 4 (64 requests, greedy): 0/64 identical. Tokens alone / GaaD: MTP 0.8B 10,274 / 7,421 (B1); MTP 35B 9,447 / 7,144 (B1); DFlash 8,983 / 6,833; n-gram 8,975 / 6,816.
- Tokenization drift example (Qwen3.5-0.8B, fp32 HF reference). After `{\n` the four allowed tokens have logits 16.88 (one space), 16.63 (three spaces, canonical), 12.37 and 12.32, a margin of 0.25. Greedy output uses about 40% more tokens than the canonical tokenization.
- Table 5 mixed traffic (0.8B, MTP, 256 requests): see claim 3. Plain-only traffic under the per-batch rule runs 0.92x (B1), 1.03x (B16) and 0.81x (B64); the authors attribute this to padding and have not profiled it.
- Table 6 host planning: 0.136 / 0.910 / 2.743 ms per step (1.0 / 1.3 / 1.2%).

|---|---|---|
| Forced tokens as draft slots (row merge/cut, host planner) | YES. Host-only logic. With llguidance builds, `llg_matcher_compute_ff_tokens` returns the forced run in one call (vendored v1.0.1 header `llguidance.h:576`). On stock GBNF, the char-level forced string needs the internal stack accessor (`src/llama-grammar.h:73 llama_grammar_get_stacks`, not public API). | YES, same host code. Planner cost in the paper is ~1% of the step. |
| Row wider than the drafter's share (K > K_M) | PARTIAL. 27B is dense and its verify slope at small n is shallow (RKA-VERIFY-TILE measures it). Grammar requests currently lose the tree in batch mode (AK-DTREE-2 guard), so a widened chain would partly recover that. | PARTIAL. A3B is MoE: each extra verify row can route to different experts and add weight bytes on a bandwidth-bound decode (uncited mechanism). Widening costs more per row than on a dense model, and the paper's own ablation shows wider rows alone lose (0.67-0.89x on 35B-A3B). |
| Logit-free accept (LM head only for MODEL + bonus rows) | PARTIAL. Saves LM-head GEMM rows and host sampler work per forced row. But our drafters are fed the target's features from verify rows (`spec_tree_resolve` comment: "the drafter is fed the accepted path's features"), so the row's hidden-state output must survive. llama.cpp's per-token output flag (`batch.add(..., true)`, server-context.cpp:899) gates outputs together, so the logits output must be split from the feature output: a port cost. | PARTIAL. LM-head weights are read once per batch (bandwidth-bound), so dropping rows saves little bandwidth. The real saving is host-side per-row sampling over the 248k vocab, which the A3B-CPU-GAP-CENSUS box treats as an outside-graph cost. Same output-flag constraint. |
| Lossless only if the mask admits exactly one token | Applies equally. Under T>0 vendor sampling (27B T1.0), accept-with-p=1 is a biased sampler wherever several tokenizations are allowed. | Same (35B T0.7). |
| Synchronous step for grammar-draft batches (§3.4) | PARTIAL. llama-server already samples and accepts on the host between decodes (verify at server-context.cpp:6531). The exception is GPU-ZOS-2 C3 overlap for DFlash pure-verify batches (`zos2_overlap_eligible`), which a grammar-draft batch would have to give up. | YES, little cost. The CPU path has no async overlap to lose, so the paper's plain-TPOT penalty mechanism mostly does not apply. Untested. |
| Canonical tokenization of forced text | Model-independent property of the grammar engine. llguidance ff tokens already trim up to ~4 tail tokens; in-house audit: 0 under-trim (SPEC-GRAM.2(4)). | Same. |

## 5. In-house state (C21 `98ff8a4`, orchestrator `1cc2043a`)

- **Speculation is NOT disabled for grammar requests.** `can_speculate()` returns `!!spec` (`tools/server/server-context.cpp:821-823 @ 98ff8a4`). Drafts stay grammar-blind: `common/speculative.cpp` and `common/speculative-tree.cpp` contain zero `grammar` references @ 98ff8a4.
- **The grammar is applied per draft row at verify time.** The exact-match chain verify (`common/sampling.cpp:660-741 @ 98ff8a4`) calls `common_sampler_sample(..., grammar_first=false)` for every row. That samples the chain, checks the token against the grammar, and resamples under the mask if invalid (`common/sampling.cpp:616-658`). At T>0 with drafter dists, `common_sampler_spec_reject_step` (`common/sampling.cpp:1038-1107`) builds the target after every constraint (`grammar_first=true`, :1047). A grammar-forbidden draft therefore has p=0 and is rejected, and the residual p−q stays correct. The in-house verify is lossless under a grammar, unlike vLLM's drop-before-verify, which the paper concedes shifts the distribution. Per SPEC-RNG.1.1 (`speculative-decoding.md`), draft-mtp never fills `spec_dists`, so A3B MTP at T>0 takes the sample-and-compare branch (`server-context.cpp:6531-6538`): accept iff the target's own masked sample equals the draft.
- **Grammar disables the fast paths and trees.** The greedy-fast and device-argmax accept fall back to the host when `grmr != nullptr` (`common/sampling.cpp:957-963`; `common_sampler_device_greedy_eligible`, :850-860). Batch-mode trees, and with them the V2 RRS sampled tree, are off for any grammar request (`server-context.cpp:5617-5619`, comment :5621-5622: "grammar stays excluded above"). The seq0 (non-batch) tree mode is not covered by that guard, and its greedy winner pick `common_dtree_greedy_accept_len` (`common/speculative-tree.cpp:336-370`) compares the raw argmax and ignores the grammar. This affects efficiency only: the accept pass then re-samples the winner under the grammar. The RRS path runs `common_sampler_tree_accept` (`common/sampling.cpp:1178-1203`) on a sampler clone that carries the grammar.
- **No forced-token drafting exists.** `common/llguidance.cpp` wraps the matcher (consume_token, compute_mask; :39-60 @ 98ff8a4) but never calls `llg_matcher_compute_ff_tokens`, although the pinned v1.0.1 C API exposes it (with `validate_tokens` and `rollback`). GaaD is **absent**. In-house it was proposed as SPEC-GRAM.5 and declined on 2026-11-03 against a 1.02-1.09x ceiling (`handoffs/active/speculative-decoding.md:811 @ f74b175`). Under the sample-and-compare MTP verify, the lossless form of GaaD is just: append the forced run to `spec_draft` and raise the `get_n_draft_max` cap (`server-context.cpp:835-869`; the "spec_tree_resolve" feature comment is at :5654). No verifier change is needed, because a deterministic draft accepted iff the target samples it has exactly the target's probability. At T>0 with DFlash2 dists, the forced token enters with q = δ (dist {tok: 1.0}) through the existing reject step.
- **Measured in-house forced share** (`speculative-decoding.md:772-799 @ f74b175`, SPEC-GRAM.2(3)/(4), llguidance replay of 5,450 local-model tool calls): XML tool-call format 8.34% trimmed (13.40% untrimmed), ceiling 1.091x, mean forced run 2.45 tokens; JSON 2.07% (flexible) / 3.84% (compact), ceiling 1.02-1.04x. SPEC-GRAM.1(2): json_schema costs draft-mtp acceptance only −1.95 pp (`speculative-decoding.md:695`), so in our lane the "drafter fights the grammar" premise is weak. The replay explicitly had **no json_schema-request outputs on disk** and stood in tool-call contents for schema-eager traffic.
- **Which production requests are grammar-constrained** (orchestrator `1cc2043a`): the frontdoor turn-1 GBNF is OFF since 2026-10-23 (`ORCHESTRATOR_TURN1_GRAMMAR`, `v11-promotion-checklist.md:127`). The chat `tools` passthrough (`src/backends/llama_server.py:966-972`) is lazy grammar for auto tool_choice. json_schema is sent through `response_format` (`src/backends/llama_server.py:1838-1845`) by typed decisions (`src/typed_decisions/runner.py:231`), review schemas (`src/proactive_delegation/review_service.py:777,942,1088,1282,1736`), REPL routing and combined ops (`src/repl_environment/routing.py:672,714`, `combined_ops.py:80,409,454`), the plan-steps schema (`src/api/routes/chat_pipeline/proactive_stage.py:328,332`), `direct_stage.py:165,218`, `consultation.py:259`, `structured_output/repair.py:1111` and `typed_decisions/judge_redundancy.py:943`. Raw GBNF is used by aggregation (`src/aggregation/facts_llm.py:163,179`, `facts_typed.py:228`). No on-disk corpus of these outputs is known. `src/inference_tap.py` is in-process only, and `_request_constraints` (`src/llm_primitives/inference.py:444-467`) only labels tap events.
- **Served path.** Production v10 builds llguidance OFF (V11-122). C-line builds carry llguidance v1.0.1. The temperatures (27B T1.0, 35B T0.7) are operator-given, with ratification `RATIFY-OFFICIAL-SAMPLING-20261124` named in `autokernel-loop.md:8580`; not separately verified.

## 7. Dive-surfaced sources (index grepped unbounded at f74b175)

- **arXiv 2605.07698**, Nie et al., "Future Validity is the Missing Statistic: From Impossibility to Φ-Estimation for Grammar-Faithful Speculative Decoding" (2026-05-08). **Absent** from the index (0 hits). It bears on GaaD's "lossless" reference point: any speculative decoder with local mask access and Leviathan rejection samples the locally projected distribution, not the grammar-conditional one. Its abstract reports a TV gap up to 0.996 on Dyck grammars with Qwen3-8B, and finite-language correction closing JSON gaps. Grammar-masked decoding (GaaD's baseline, and our in-house verify) is therefore itself not the grammar-conditional distribution. GaaD's tokenization drift is a second, separate shift. Only the abstract was read. Recommendation: **ingest-and-dive (low-medium)**. It would settle whether "lossless vs grammar-masked decoding" is the right acceptance target for any A2 design, and whether its OneStep correction (<1% throughput overhead per the abstract) matters for our finite JSON schemas.
- **vllm-torchtpu PR #3321** (open, head 4082e8a). Absent ("vllm-torchtpu" 0 hits). It is the paper's companion implementation by the same authors. Recommendation: **decline a separate entry**. Record it in this entry's notes, not as corroboration.
