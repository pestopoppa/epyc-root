## 2. Key claims
0. **Gumbel coupling dominates Weighted MinHash, and the exact match probability is a closed form: CONFIRMED.**
   - Pr[a=b] = sum over j with min(p_j,q_j)>0 of 1 / sum_i max(p_i/p_j, q_i/q_j) (Theorem 3). It is at least the Weighted MinHash value, which is at least (1-D_TV)/(1+D_TV).
   - For two-item supports it equals 1-D_TV exactly. Their 3-item example gives 2/3 for Gumbel against 7/12 for Weighted MinHash.
   - Anchors: abstract ("an equally simple protocol based on Gumbel sampling offers a Pareto improvement"); §4 (n = 2 case "Gumbel sampling has exactly the same collision probability as that of the optimal coupling").
1. **Coupled-draw verification cannot beat rejection sampling per position; the premise of the dice dive's A2 is overturned: OVERTURNED (premise), CONFIRMED (bound).**
   - Standard speculative decoding already attains the optimal-coupling value 1-D_TV, because the drafter communicates its whole distribution to the verifier. Every other coupling, communication-free or not, is at or below that value; 1-D_TV is the maximum by the definition of total variation (Eq. 1). Communication-free coupling can only reach (1-D_TV)/(1+D_TV) in the worst case (Theorem 2).
   - So the paper's own object, a drafter-invariant scheme, buys invariance at a price (up to ~2x fewer correct draft tokens in the worst case). It does not buy acceptance. The temperature dependence the dice dive asked about does not rescue it: the bound is distribution-by-distribution.
   - Anchors: §1 "it is possible to sample from a near-optimal coupling without any communication at all"; §1.2 "improving on the bound in Fact 1 is not possible in the worst-case"; §5 "Alice actually communicates the entire distribution she used to sample".
2. **Drafter invariance: CONFIRMED (one query).** Table 1 uses Gemma-2-27B as target and Gemma-2-9B and Gemma-2-2B as drafters on one prompt. Drafter-invariant decoding returns the same text for both drafters; standard speculative decoding returns three different responses depending on the drafter. Anchor: §5.1 "the tokens generated by the large model remain the same regardless of the drafter model".
3. **Empirical support for "nearly matches standard speculative decoding": PARTIAL.** The only measurement is Figure 2: 32 response tokens per query from Gemma-2-27B with Gemma-2-2B as the drafter, 20,000 seeds each, collision probability against D_TV. Gumbel is above Weighted MinHash on all points. There is no tokens/s, no end-to-end speedup, no multi-token acceptance length, and no sampling temperature stated (NOT-FOUND-IN-SOURCE). Anchor: §5.1 "We issue queries to the gemma-2-27b-it model, then generate a sequence of 32 response tokens...".
4. **The mechanism is a drafter-before-verifier protocol with no feedback: CONFIRMED.** The drafter must sample before the verifier, so only drafter-to-verifier information exists; standard speculative decoding uses it to send the full drafter distribution, which makes the output depend on the drafter. Anchor: §5 "Alice has to generate her sample before Bob, so the setting inherently does not allow communication from Bob to Alice."

## 3. Reported results

## 8. Contradiction / adversarial pass
- The paper does not show an end-to-end speedup, a temperature, or a comparison with standard speculative sampling in tokens per second; "nearly matches" rests on Figure 2 and on the theory.
- The authors state the cost of invariance: up to a ~2x reduction in correct draft tokens in the worst case, with real language-model distributions tending to do better.
- v4 footnote discloses prior proofs (Angel and Spinka; Li and Anantharam); the Gumbel result is not original to this paper.
- Restatement note: the dice paper's use of the same coupling is not corroboration of any speed number here; no figure is carried across.
