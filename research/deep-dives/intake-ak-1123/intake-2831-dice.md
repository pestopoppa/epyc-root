## 1. Stage-1 fields
- **Title**: Speculative Decoding Is Less Speculative Than You Think (method name DICE, Draw-Informed Candidate Selection)
- **Authors**: Guanning Zeng, Andrea Zanette (Carnegie Mellon University)
- **Version**: v1, 20 Nov 2026, cs.AI
- **Categories**: speculative_decoding, inference_serving
- **Novelty**: medium. Shared-randomness coupling in speculative decoding is prior art that the paper itself cites: Daliri et al. 2025 (drafter-invariant SD), Rowan et al. 2025 and Kobus & Gündüz 2025. DICE's new contribution is using the pre-drawn Gumbel and uniform variables to *allocate* the tree-node budget and the prefetch-cache budget. Earlier work used them only to couple samples.
- **Relevance**: medium.
  - DICE gives zero gain at T = 0 ("both methods verify greedily and run at the same speed").
  - Our tree path (DTREE) is greedy-only and is not served, because T16 only reaches parity with linear.
  - Production samples at low temperature: 0.1–0.3 for most roles in orchestration/model_registry.yaml @ orchestrator 1a10541b (L2256 frontdoor 0.3, L2341 coder_escalation 0.2, L2409 ingest_long_context 0.2, L2511 architect_general 0.1, L2607 architect_critic 0.1, L2690 worker_general 0.3, L2736 worker_math 0.0). DF2-FT-1, however, describes the :8083 traffic as "temp-1 agentic traffic" (speculative-decoding.md:2250 @ d785b6d).
  - DICE's gain grows with T, so at our temperatures it is small (inferred; the paper gives no number below T = 1 in its text).
- **credibility_score**: 2. Not peer-reviewed (0); recent (+1); CMU with a known speculative-decoding/RL author (Zanette) (+1); no commercial bias (0); no independent corroboration (0).
- **Verdict**: worth_investigating. The method is training-free, lossless (proved in Appendix B) and portable. Its value for us depends on sampled-mode verification. In-house, a position-keyed RNG already exists on an experimental branch (SPEC-RNG.1, recorded as DO-NOT-CARRY). A bounded probe can answer whether DICE pays at our temperatures.

## 2. Key claims
0. **Mechanism: CONFIRMED.**
   - The verification randomness is drawn before drafting: Gumbel vectors per absolute output position, and acceptance uniforms. The draws are generated by a counter-based hash of (request seed, absolute position, vocab index) and shared by the drafter and the verifier.
   - The tree builder scores child v of node x as r̂ = softmax((α·d_x + T·g_{|x|+1})/σ). It accumulates path scores ŵ, expands by ŵ and keeps the top-N nodes by ŵ.
   - Nodes at the same depth share that depth's Gumbel vector.
   - α and σ are fit once per target–drafter pair by MLE on about 2,000 observed nodes (1–3 prompts). The plug-in α = σ = 1 already gives +9–22% acceptance length.
   - Anchors: page §3 "pre-samples and synchronizes verification randomness across drafting and verification"; §3.3 "We generate verification randomness on demand using a deterministic, counter-based hash indexed by the request seed and absolute token position".
1. **"Up to 24.6% throughput over strong baselines": CONFIRMED, with scope.**
   - The 24.6% is one cell of Table 1: Llama-3.1-8B target with an EAGLE-3 drafter, HF Transformers backend, SPEED-Bench, T = 1, concurrency 1, H200, against EAGLE-3's own tree method (53.1 → 66.2 tok/s).
   - Range over 18 model–backend configurations: 4.3–21.5% on Spec-Bench and 5.6–24.6% on SPEED-Bench.
   - Production engines (SGLang/vLLM, MTP and EAGLE-4) gain 4.97–20.88%.
   - Hardware *is* stated (H200). The steering said it was not.
   - Anchors: "by 4.3–21.5% on Spec-Bench and 5.6–24.6% on SPEED-Bench"; "Unless otherwise stated, we sample at T = 1 T=1 and serve one request at a time on H200 GPUs."; Table 1 row text "66.2 66.2 2.05 2.05 3.62 3.62 (+24.62%)".
2. **Greedy (T = 0): OVERTURNED as a lever.**
   - At T = 0 the Gumbel term vanishes (T·g = 0) and DICE reduces to the baseline tree. The paper says both methods run at the same speed.
   - DICE is a sampled-mode technique only.
   - Anchor: "At T = 0 T=0 , both methods verify greedily and run at the same speed."
3. **Preserves the target distribution: CONFIRMED (Appendix B).**
   - Proposition 1: for any tree rule that depends on the prefix, the drafter and the draws, the emitted sequence equals autoregressive Gumbel-max sampling under the same draws. The tree changes only how many tokens a round emits.
   - Proposition 2: the SSD prefetch policy reads the draws only to choose which drafts to prepare, so each round remains a standard speculative-sampling round.
   - Requirement: the target must sample by Gumbel-max with the shared position-keyed draws, and unused draws must carry over between rounds.
   - Anchor: "The draft tree may depend on the draws in any way, but it only determines how many tokens each round emits, not which tokens."
4. **No drafter retraining: CONFIRMED.** Only the two calibration scalars are fit, online. Anchors: "Without additional drafter training or changes to the target sampling distribution"; "We calibrate these two parameters only once for each target–drafter pair."
5. **The gain comes from the synchronized draws, not from noise: CONFIRMED (Figure 5a, text).** With shuffled (independent) draws, acceptance length falls 11–14%; with synchronized draws it rises 11–24%. Anchor: "This lowers the acceptance length by 11–14% relative to a drafter that sees no draws. With synchronized draws, DICE raises it by 11–24%."

## 3. Reported results (as stated)
- **Tree decoding.** Table 1: H200, T = 1, concurrency 1; 16 target–drafter pairs over 18 model–backend configurations (OPT-Tree standalone drafters, EAGLE-2/3/4, MTP). Throughput gain is 4.3–21.5% on Spec-Bench and 5.6–24.6% on SPEED-Bench. Example cells:
  - Qwen3.8-27B/MTP (SGLang): +6.87% Spec-Bench, +9.22% SPEED-Bench (τ 3.39 → 4.13).
  - Qwen3.8-27B/EAGLE-4 (vLLM): +14.91% / +20.88%.
  - Qwen3.6-35B-A3B/MTP (SGLang): +10.86% / +10.20%.
- **Prefetching.** Table 2: H200, T = 1, concurrency 1, against vanilla SSD on four pairs. Cache hit rate rises from 45.6–67.6% to 69.8–80.7%, giving 4.0–13.7% higher throughput.
- **Concurrency.** Figure 4: Qwen3.8-27B on SPEED-Bench, concurrency 1–64. DICE leads the strongest baseline by 2–18% (SGLang) and 8–20% (vLLM). At concurrency 64, DFlash2 and DSpark fall below AR while DICE stays 1.19x / 1.28x over AR.
- **Budget.** EAGLE-4 needs 64 nodes to reach the acceptance length DICE gets with 16 (Figure 5c).
- **Temperature.** Figure 3 shows the advantage growing with T. Numbers are given only for T = 0 (parity) and T = 1.
- **Run statistics.** No repeat counts or confidence intervals are reported.

## 4. Performance-principles table (mechanism and cost model)

**1. Position-keyed counter-hash draws (seed, position, vocab id).**
- Transfers to MI210 gfx90a: YES. A cheap device hash. The paper fuses it into candidate selection and keeps it CUDA-graph-capturable.
- Transfers to Zen 5 EPYC: YES. Host-side hash, negligible cost.
- In-house state: SPEC-RNG.1 implemented SplitMix64(seed, absolute position, role) on `experimental/spec-rng-argv-20261022` (261db6c7e), with a DO-NOT-CARRY decision (speculative-decoding.md:637-645). It keys a uniform, not a Gumbel per vocab id.

**2. Gumbel-perturbed tree scoring r̂ = softmax((αd + Tg)/σ).**
- MI210: YES. The change is host code in `common_dtree_build_greedy` (common/speculative-tree.h:37-45, speculative-tree.cpp @ a6a32c1). It needs Gumbel values only for the top-K candidates at each depth.
- Zen 5: YES, same code.
- Benefit appears only at T > 0. Our tree accept is greedy-only today (`common_dtree_greedy_accept_len`, speculative-tree.cpp:336 @ a6a32c1; device path requires `common_sampler_device_greedy_eligible`, server-context.cpp:5640-5643).

**3. Target samples by Gumbel-max with the same draws; matching verification walks the tree.**
- MI210: PARTIAL. Needs a new sampler mode: Gumbel-max over the post-truncation logits with keyed draws, plus a tree walk that compares the sampled token with the children. Port cost is moderate (sampler, tree accept, draw carry-over).
- Zen 5: PARTIAL. Same code as on the MI210.
- In-house note from SPEC-RNG.1.1: draft-mtp already uses exact-match verify with one draw per emitted token (common/sampling.cpp:691-725 at that branch), while DFlash2 uses a rejection path.

**4. DICE on a chain (one child per node).**
- MI210: PARTIAL. For DFlash2 and MTP chains, the token at each depth becomes argmax(αd + Tg) instead of argmax d. This is a zero-cost change, but it requires the matching/Gumbel verifier from row 3 instead of DFlash2's rejection sampling.
- Zen 5: PARTIAL, same.
- The paper gives no chain-only number in its text.

**5. Uniform-draw acceptance model (max-entropy ratio) for SSD prefetch.**
- MI210: NO today. We have no asynchronous prefetch pipeline. SSD is intake-102.
- Zen 5: NO today, same reason.
- Port cost would be the whole SSD pipeline, so this is out of scope until an SSD box exists.

**6. Online MLE calibration of (α, σ).**
- MI210: YES. Concave 2-parameter fit, few gradient steps.
- Zen 5: YES.

## 5. In-house state
- **DTREE device tree accept** (fold `c9bc20a57` in C19, default off) took T16 from −4.1% to parity with linear: e2e T16/L 0.9966, inside the A/A band. "The DTREE reopen trigger does not fire" (speculative-decoding.md:1208-1220 @ d785b6d). DTREE-GDN-BATCH is the open lever on verify cost (L1221).
- **DTREE-TREE-QUALITY** already ships a probability-greedy best-first tree, `LLAMA_DTREE_POLICY=greedy` (`418cafb20`, C18). This is exactly the EAGLE-style score that DICE perturbs. The offline simulation puts its best policy at +3.66% over static T16 (L1050-1054).
- **Mapping onto T12/T16.** DICE would change the node scores in `common_dtree_build_greedy`, adding T·g at each depth's Gumbel vector. It would also replace `common_dtree_greedy_accept_len` with a Gumbel-max tree walk when T > 0. Under a fixed node budget (T12/T16) the selected set stays a valid tree (Appendix A: ŵ is monotone along paths).
  - Our DFlash2 per-position distributions do not depend on the branch (block drafter). All nodes at one depth therefore share both d and g, and DICE then only reorders siblings by d + Tg/α. The tree builder's depth-versus-width split would change; the drafter call would not.
- **Production regime.** Temperatures are 0.1–0.3 (see §1), and no tree is served. The DICE-relevant path is therefore the chain DFlash2/MTP verifier in sampled mode.
- **Upstream DICE code**: none published (no repo link in the page).

## 8. Contradiction / adversarial pass
- **No limitations section** in the paper.
- **No CIs or repeat counts** for throughput, so single-digit gains (e.g. +4.34%) carry unknown run noise.
- **Contested baselines.**
  - Baselines are each drafter's own tree method (OPT-Tree, EAGLE-2/3, EAGLE-4 cost-aware sizing).
  - Sequoia and SpecTr appear only in related work. There is no head-to-head against hardware-aware optimal trees, and none against prior coupling methods (Daliri et al.).
  - The concurrency claim that "DFlash2 falls below AR at concurrency 64" is figure-only. It runs our served drafter family on a different engine, so I treat it as unverified.
- **T = 1 is the headline setting.** At T = 0 the gain is zero, and the paper gives no in-text numbers between T = 0 and T = 1. Our production 0.1–0.3 regime is therefore uncharacterized.
- **Losslessness depends on the target sampling by Gumbel-max with the shared keyed draws.** With top-p/min-p truncation that is still valid if the draws apply to the truncated logits (my reasoning; not discussed in the paper).
- **No reproductions found.**
