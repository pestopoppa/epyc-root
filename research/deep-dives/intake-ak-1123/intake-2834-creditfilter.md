- **Authors**: Hanqing Zhao, Liuyue Xie, Rong-Cheng Tu, Xu Zhang, Davide Modolo, Dacheng Tao (Nanyang Technological University; Amazon AGI)

## 2. Key claims

**Claim 0 — the weighting rule. CONFIRMED (read from the paper and the released code).**
Each token's advantage is multiplied by a non-negative coefficient built from the token's *within-response* entropy rank. Rank q = r/T in (0,1], where r is the descending-entropy rank among the T tokens of that response (q near 0 = most uncertain token). Kernel k = max(2^(-q/lambda_c), eps_s): exponential, halving every lambda rank-fraction. Class width lambda_c is chosen by the response's verifier outcome: lambda_+ = 0.10 (correct), lambda_- = 0.14 (wrong), rounded from measured envelope widths 0.111 / 0.155 on Qwen3-8B-Base. Floor eps_+ = 0.1 on correct (positive-advantage) responses only, chosen so the mean weights of the two classes match (class masses 0.196 vs 0.200); no floor on wrong responses. The full coefficient is w = g_i * [(1 - m_gov) + m_gov * k], with difficulty damping g_i = (1 - pass_rate_of_group)^gamma, gamma = 1, applied to positive-advantage responses only (code default CF_DAMPING_NEG = 0), and an entropy-gated fallback m_gov = clip((H_hi - Hbar)/(H_hi - H_lo), 0, 1) with H_lo/H_hi = 1.5/3.0 nats on the batch-mean token entropy (above 3 nats the window is replaced by uniform weights). No token is deleted: it is a soft reweighting. Correct responses keep a 10% floor on all tokens; wrong responses get near-zero credit on low-entropy tokens (2^(-q/0.14) is 0.5 at q = 0.14 and 0.016 at q = 0.84). Entropy comes from the same softmax the trainer already computes, so no extra forward pass. Calibration of lambda is the exception: it was measured offline on base-policy rollouts with an instrument that branches continuations (3.58 million continuations, 2.97 billion generated tokens, 16 GPUs, 6.2 h) and the paper reports widths range 0.071 to 0.267 across eight models, so lambda is per-model; the CreditFilter-D variant re-estimates width online (EMA alpha = 0.1) at no extra cost.

**Claim 1 — "+7.7 MATH500, +10.5 AIME24, +9.1 AIME25 over DAPO (3-seed average)". PARTIAL.**
- The numbers are real and are absolute accuracy points (mean@32, late average of checkpoints 350/375/400): CreditFilter 0.860 / 0.326 / 0.258 vs DAPO 0.783 / 0.221 / 0.167. Model: Qwen3-8B-Base only. The three "seeds" are *data-order seeds* (201, 300, 400), not independent initialisations.
- The MATH500 margin is not a stable effect. Per seed, CreditFilter minus DAPO on MATH500 is +0.046, -0.027, +0.212. The mean +0.077 is carried by one DAPO run (seed 400) that scored 0.660; on seed 300 DAPO beats CreditFilter. Paired std is 0.122, larger than the mean 0.077. On AIME24/25 the margin is consistent in sign (+0.094/+0.023/+0.197 and +0.100/+0.026/+0.148) and the paper correctly notes each CreditFilter seed is above every DAPO seed on both AIME sets, but AIME sets are n = 30 problems each.
- The DAPO baseline is the authors' own reproduction at a reduced budget (128 prompts/step, 400 steps, 20,480 cap, top-p 0.7 eval). The paper itself notes Wang et al. published a DAPO number of 89.2 / 33.3 / 25.4 on the same model at 512 prompts/step; their DAPO row is 0.830 / 0.244 / 0.177 on the first seed. CreditFilter-D (0.887 / 0.367 / 0.268) lands at or above that published DAPO level on AIME and slightly below on MATH500, under different eval protocols (top-p 0.7, 20,480 cap vs the published setup), so the cells are not directly comparable.

**Claim 2 — "no extra cost". PARTIAL, strongly qualified.**
No extra *forward pass*, yes. But the method trains on 2.1x DAPO's tokens at equal steps (6.04e9 vs 2.87e9 optimizer tokens) in 2.1x the wall time (about 145 h vs 70 h on 16 H100), because responses are longer; reasoning up to the first stated answer is 2.8 to 4.3 times DAPO's (MATH500 2,556 vs 905 tokens). At a 4k decode budget CreditFilter is *worse* than DAPO (0.779 vs 0.827 MATH500) and only leads from 8k up. The authors' controls: DAPO continued to step 600 does not improve (0.832 / 0.256 / 0.206), and at equal training tokens CreditFilter's step-300 checkpoint (0.850 / 0.299 / 0.250) beats DAPO step 575 (0.829 / 0.256 / 0.204), but those equal-token comparisons are single-seed.

**Claim 3 — "both parts matter: rank within response, and the calibrated window width". PARTIAL.**
Within-response ranking vs batch ranking is well supported by ablation (batch-level rank 0.826 / 0.264 / 0.194, within 2 points of DAPO; the 80/20 selection rule gains 8 to 13 points when the cut is per-response). Width matters (doubling to 0.20/0.28 loses 9 to 15 points). But the *exponential, envelope-calibrated shape* is not shown to beat a plain within-response top-20% window: over three seeds the top-20% window is 0.817 / 0.347 / 0.261 vs CreditFilter 0.860 / 0.326 / 0.258; paired CreditFilter minus top-20% is +0.043 +/- 0.059 (MATH500), -0.021 +/- 0.017 (AIME24), -0.003 +/- 0.011 (AIME25). The paper itself says "the exact shape matters less than the width". What the exponential buys is shorter generation and fewer unanswered samples (17 to 28% fewer tokens). The defensible ingredient is *entropy-ordered credit redistribution inside each response with a calibrated concentration*, not the measured-envelope theory.

**Claim 4 — code is public. CONFIRMED.** https://github.com/yoctta/CreditFilter, Apache-2.0, HEAD 539873250e746edce23c91d3cf32578d7e24659f. It is a copy of verl 0.8.0.dev plus a `creditfilter/` package (`credit_filter.py` about 384 lines, baselines, token signals), launchers for every arm of the paper, eval scripts and the measurement instrument. Default launch assumes a Ray cluster of 8-GPU nodes, 2 nodes by default (`ACTOR_NNODES` = 2), `HW_PROFILE` h100 or b200, python 3.12, torch 2.10.0, transformers 5.3.0, vllm 0.18.0.

## 3. Reported results (as the source states them)

- Setup: Qwen3-8B-Base, DAPO-17k prompts (17,917 boxed-answer problems), 400 steps, 128 prompts x 16 responses per step, mini-batch 32 prompts (4 optimizer updates/step), AdamW lr 1e-6 constant, no KL, no entropy bonus, token-mean loss, response cap 20,480, clip 0.2/0.27 (DAPO 0.28). Hardware: three GPU types (H100, H200, B200); CreditFilter, GRPO and DAPO timing runs were on the same 16 H100 GPUs. Eval: vLLM 0.18.0 bf16, 32 samples/problem, temperature 1.0, top-p 0.7, 20,480 new tokens; MATH500 n = 500, AIME24 n = 30, AIME25 n = 30.
- Seed 201 (Table 1, late average): CreditFilter 0.876 / 0.338 / 0.277; CreditFilter-D 0.887 / 0.367 / 0.268; GRPO 0.833 / 0.263 / 0.184; DAPO 0.830 / 0.244 / 0.177; 80/20 mask (released script) 0.827 / 0.302 / 0.236; SC-GRPO 0.838 / 0.269 / 0.168; STARE 0.824 / 0.189 / 0.156; base model 0.507 / 0.055 / 0.047.
- Three seeds (Table 2): CreditFilter 0.860 +/- 0.025, 0.326 +/- 0.042, 0.258 +/- 0.035; DAPO 0.783 +/- 0.107, 0.221 +/- 0.050, 0.167 +/- 0.032.
- Training tokens over 400 steps (1e9): CreditFilter 6.04, CreditFilter-D 5.06, GRPO 3.90, DAPO 2.87, 80/20 mask 5.21, top-20% window 6.88. Wall time on 16 H100: about 145 h (CreditFilter), 80 h (GRPO), 70 h (DAPO).
- Mechanism evidence: fork probability C = 1 - sum(pi^2) decays exponentially in within-response entropy rank; top 20% of ranks hold 68% (correct) / 63% (wrong) of measured forking value; fork consequence roughly flat except about 6x in the top 0.25% of ranks (about two tokens per response). Entropy-window update moves the policy 1.4 to 2.3x further in KL than a uniform update without moving parameters further.
- Authors' stated limitations: one base model, one prompt set, 30-problem AIME sets, a 20,480-token cap; ablations and reproduced baselines are single runs; longer responses; theory tested on the base policy only; propositions exclude the overlong penalty.

## 8. Contradiction / adversarial pass

- Seed fragility: the MATH500 headline rests on one collapsed DAPO run (0.660) and reverses on seed 300; paired std exceeds the mean. AIME margins are more stable but n = 30.
- Baseline strength: own-reproduced DAPO, reduced budget, a different eval protocol from the published DAPO numbers; the paper says as much and gives both sets.
- Cost: at equal steps it trains 2.1x tokens in 2.1x time and generates much longer reasoning (2.8 to 4.3x to first answer); worse at a 4k decode budget; 11 to 16% of step-400 samples of the no-damping ablation reach the cap unanswered, so the gains depend on the damping and fallback stabilisers.
- The measured-envelope theory is partly after the fact: the propositions need a rank look-ahead assumption that holds approximately, the window omits the value factor, and a plain top-20% within-response window matches it on AIME.
- Scope: one model, one prompt set, math only, single-turn; no agent, no LoRA, no model other than Qwen3-8B-Base trained.
- All corroboration in the paper is the authors' own; no independent replication was found (none in the intake index; no external blog restating the number was fetched).
