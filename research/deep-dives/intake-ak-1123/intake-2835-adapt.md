## 2. Key claims

**Claim 0 — off-the-shelf models are miscalibrated about asking an advisor. CONFIRMED (Table 1).** With the same ask_advisor tool and Gemini 3.7 Flash as advisor, Qwen3-8B asks 0.12 / 0.88 / 0.11 times per episode on TextWorld / LCB-stdin / GAIA-Text and its oracle-ceiling success rate is 86 / 52 / 62; Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite over-asks (3.2 / 2.25 / 4.14). Prompting changes ask frequency, not quality of timing (Neutral vs Nudged).

**Claim 1 — the mechanism (three stages from the policy's own rollouts). CONFIRMED.**
(1) SFT warm-start (rejection sampling, K = 8 generations per training task) on the policy's own successful trajectories with the ask_advisor tool available. (2) Counterfactual preference mining: at probed steps (a random quarter of the step horizon, biased toward syntax errors, tool failures, timeouts, loops) roll the policy forward K times under "ask here" and "act here"; ask-advantage A(t) = p_ask(t) - p_noask(t). Ask pairs where p_ask > 0.6 and A > 0.3 (chosen = call advisor); Abstain pairs where p_noask > 0.6 and A <= 0 (chosen = act directly); Follow pairs (advice followed to a win vs advice followed to a loss). DPO with LoRA, 2 epochs, lr 1e-5, beta 0.1; advice tokens masked. (3) Cost-constrained GRPO: maximise E[win] subject to E[n_ask] <= c, relaxed to a per-episode reward R_i = win_i - lambda * n_ask_i with lambda a shadow price solved by dual ascent (lambda <- max(0, lambda + eta (mean_n_ask - c))); the budget c never enters the reward directly; KL anchor to the DPO policy. The value of K for the replay is NOT-FOUND-IN-SOURCE. Training hardware and GPU-hours: NOT-FOUND-IN-SOURCE (only "limited compute", LoRA rank 32, alpha 64, 16,384 max sequence). Training needs advisor calls (replay and RL rollouts) as well as test time; the one cost given is $0.00193 per advisor call.

**Claim 2 — "TextWorld 24.5 -> 77.3, LCB 21.8 -> 56.0, GAIA 17.5 -> 49.3 at 1.0 to 2.1 calls per task". PARTIAL (numbers real, baseline choice flatters, LCB is not a win).** The "from" values are the Neutral-prompt untrained harness. The same untrained policy under the Nudged prompt is 28.2 / 35.1 / 19.4, so the LCB gain over the best prompt-only condition is +20.9, not +34.2. On LCB ADAPT (56.01) is below the AgentRL baseline (58.3); the authors say so and explain it by LCB's dense unit-test verifier. Asks per episode 1.09 / 1.02 / 2.12 confirmed. ADAPT remains far below advisor-only: 77.3 vs 90.8 (TextWorld), 56.0 vs 79.5 (LCB), 49.3 vs 77.8 (GAIA). Test sets: 109 / 134 / 103 episodes; one episode is about 0.9 to 1.0 points.

**Claim 3 — "closes 70 to 85% of the gap to an oracle that perfectly times when to ask". PARTIAL.** Arithmetic confirmed against the Nudged baseline: TextWorld (77.32 - 28.2)/(86 - 28.2) = 0.85; GAIA (49.25 - 19.4)/(62 - 19.4) = 0.70. (Against Neutral: 0.86 and 0.71.) Qualifications: the Oracle is a hindsight ceiling computed by counterfactual replay on the base Qwen3-8B policy, so it is "oracle timing of the base policy", not an upper bound on achievable success: on LCB both ADAPT (56.0) and AgentRL (58.3) exceed it (52). The paper defines the oracle inconsistently: section 3.3 says "single most valuable step", section 4.2 says the best ask-or-act decision at each candidate step, and the oracle averages 2.8 asks/ep on TextWorld, which is not a single ask. Probing covers only a quarter of steps.

**Claim 4 — learned help-seeking transfers to an unseen advisor. PARTIAL.** Swapping Gemini 3.7 Flash for Claude Opus 4.8 or DeepSeek V4 Flash (unseen in training): TextWorld 77.3 -> 79 / 68, LCB 56.0 -> 62 / 53, GAIA 49.3 -> 57 / 42. These rows are single-seed (the main results average three seeds); the training advisor was in the middle of the capability range; the gain over the untrained policy persists, but the paper does not test an advisor much weaker than the SLM.

**Claim 5 — operator steering "hardware-agnostic". NOT-FOUND-IN-SOURCE.** The paper never states training or inference hardware. "Hardware-agnostic" is the operator's inference (the method is software: LoRA, DPO, GRPO). The released-code statement exists ("We release our code and training splits"), but no repository URL appears in page.txt.

## 5. In-house state (orchestrator HEAD 1a10541b, read-only; lane head d785b6d)

What decides small -> large escalation today (no learned "when to ask"; the decision is rule-, quality-detector- and memory-driven):

1. **Failure-driven rule policy.** `EscalationPolicy.decide` (src/orchestration/escalation.py:L227-L350 @ 1a10541b): format errors retry only; schema errors retry then escalate only on a capability-gap error signature; early abort escalates immediately; other errors retry up to `max_retries`, then escalate to `Role.escalates_to()`; the top role fails. Targets come from `_ESCALATION_MAP` (src/roles.py:L532 ff. @ 1a10541b): workers, toolrunner and frontdoor -> coder_escalation; coder_escalation / thinking_reasoning / architect_critic / ingest_long_context -> architect_general (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on CPU after the 2026-09-27 swap); architect_general terminal. The `EscalationContext` also carries risk and difficulty bands (escalation.py:L77-L125).
2. **Quality-detector re-answer.** `/v1` and `/chat` re-answer with coder_escalation (or the pinned consultant architect_general) when `generation_monitor` flags the answer; opt-in per request via `x_escalation` and flag `v1_escalation` default off (src/api/routes/v1_escalation.py:L1-L60, constants at L88-L93 @ 1a10541b). The doc states reviewing every answer was net-harmful (-38.3 per 100) and was dropped (RI-18c).
3. **Model-initiated escalation exists as a REPL tool**: `_escalate(reason, target_role)` sets `_escalation_requested` (src/repl_environment/routing.py:L303-L319 @ 1a10541b). This is the nearest thing to ADAPT's ask_advisor tool, but it is a *hand-off* (control passes up: the escalation chain re-runs the task at the higher role), not advice returned as an observation with the small model keeping control. It is ignored by `FrontdoorNode` in `/chat` (v1_escalation.py:L50-L56) and honoured by the coder node (src/graph/nodes.py:L445).
4. **Advisory consult exists for edit transactions**: `log_consult` (orchestration/repl_memory/progress_logger.py:L580-L607) and `review_before_commit_consult` (default off). J17 live A/B (lane: eval-tower-program.md:L1766; routing-program.md:L810-L816): on the simple BEP slice consult tied baseline (40/50 vs 40/50) with +22.6% p50 latency and 0 reruns; on the high-risk edit slice it rose 35/50 -> 40/50 with 15 reruns. This is already an under/over-asking result in ADAPT's vocabulary: blanket consult is wasteful, selective consult helps.
5. **Reward side (MemRL / Q-scorer).** `compute_reward` (orchestration/repl_memory/q_reward.py:L117-L190 @ 1a10541b): base success 1.0 / failure -0.5, gate failure -0.1 each, **escalation penalty a fixed 0.15 per escalation** (`escalation_penalty = len(escalations) * 0.15`, q_reward.py:L173), plus a default-off correctness-gated duration/cost penalty. That is ADAPT's R = win - lambda * n_ask with lambda hard-coded at 0.15 and in reward units, not a price solved against a budget.
6. **Routing memory.** `retrieve_for_escalation` (orchestration/repl_memory/retriever.py:L868-L875) embeds the failure context and picks the highest-Q action; the difficulty signal shows zero predictive spread (escalation rates 62.2 / 60.7 / 62.2% across easy / medium / hard; routing-program.md:L79).
7. **Hardware/trigger gates**: the thesis experiment (thesis-experiment-orchestrator-vs-strongest-model.md:L13, CURRENT-CAMPAIGN.md:L90-L91) is parked until the autopilot has trained escalation on the swapped stack, which waits on the second MI210; DAR-3/DAR-6 FROZEN, DAR-LAT frozen with an unfreeze trigger (routing-program.md:L7, L64).

Can the "when to ask" signal be produced without training? Partly: (a) as an evaluation protocol, yes (asks per episode, share of episodes asking, Neutral vs Nudged tool description, oracle-timing ceiling from counterfactual replay) on the existing consult/escalate primitives; (b) as a reward-shaping term, yes: replace the constant 0.15 by a price solved by dual ascent against a target ask budget, using logged episodes; (c) a *learned* policy that internalises ask timing in the small model's weights needs the training build and is not obtainable by prompting (the paper's Neutral vs Nudged and intake-2539's executor nudge both show prompts shift frequency, not timing quality).

## 8. Contradiction / adversarial pass

- Baseline framing: the headline "from" numbers are the Neutral prompt; the fairer prompt-only baseline (Nudged) is higher on LCB by 13.3 points.
- LCB does not support the thesis: AgentRL (58.3) beats ADAPT (56.0), and both exceed the paper's own "oracle" (52), so that oracle is not an upper bound and the 70 to 85% figure is only for TextWorld and GAIA.
- The oracle definition is inconsistent between section 3.3 and section 4.2, and it is computed by rollout verification on the test split with foresight; "closes X% of the gap" is therefore a diagnostic ratio, not a bound.
- Advisor strength and contamination: the advisor (Gemini 3.7 Flash, thinking on) reads the full trajectory and solves 90.8 / 79.5 / 77.8 alone; ADAPT reaches roughly 85% / 70% / 63% of advisor-only on the three benchmarks (77.3/90.8, 56.0/79.5, 49.3/77.8). The LCB contamination guard filters by contest date relative to the *policy's* cutoff (the paper asserts a Qwen3 cutoff before 2024-08-01); the advisor's cutoff is not addressed, so the advisor may have seen the problems.
- Statistical power: 103 to 134 test episodes; no standard deviations for three-seed means; advisor-swap rows single-seed; the c = 1 and c = 4 budget points are illustrative, not measured; baselines AgentRL / Step-DPO / RFT are the authors' own re-implementations.
- Scope: one 8B policy, LoRA only, environment-verifiable tasks only; harness for LCB-stdin is the authors' own (so numbers are not comparable to public LiveCodeBench leaderboards); the advisor is a hosted frontier model, not a local Qwen.
- Corroboration status: the under-asking finding is echoed by intake-2541 (Sonnet 5.5 consulted 0/20 at high effort) and intake-2539 (a nudge helped only a weak executor); those are different sources measuring a related effect, not replications of ADAPT's numbers. No independent reproduction of ADAPT was found.
