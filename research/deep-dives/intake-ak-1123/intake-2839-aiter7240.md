## 2. Key claims

0. **Enablement, priority: installation enables nothing; `configure(*, source, enable=())` is the one way to enable; the environment can only deny; priority is enable-list order, first-accept-wins, with no provider-declared priority. CONFIRMED.** `source` is mandatory and recorded in every routing decision. The single environment control is `AITER_PLUGINS=0`, which outranks `configure()`. An earlier four-variable env design was removed after implementation because an environment variable cannot name its owner and its disable/enable names were ambiguous. Selection was later allowed to follow a reported measurement (claim 3) while eligibility stayed with people.
   - Anchors (page): `aiter.plugin.configure(*, source, enable=()) — the one way to enable a Provider.`; `The environment can only take routing away, never grant it`; `Answered: the enable list order is the preference order , first-accept-wins, and a Provider declares no priority of its own.`
1. **Provenance: a selected candidate has immutable provenance known before execution. CONFIRMED.** Fields: provider name, kernel/candidate id, artifact digest, op-contract version, provider-reported qualification or evidence reference. The qualification is opaque and is "provenance, not AITER certification". Not in the field list (read from the PR text, not the RFC list): `config_source`, which names the configuration layer that authorised the route and is recorded on every decision.
   - Anchors (page): `A selected candidate has immutable provenance known before execution: Provider name; kernel or candidate ID; artifact digest; op-contract version;`; `Provider-reported qualification is provenance, not AITER certification.`
2. **Observability: one frozen Decision record is read four ways (`last_decision()`, `status()`, `explain(op, *args)` as a dry run that executes nothing, `decision_counts()` / `on_decision(cb)` plus a route-change log); knowing which kernel served a call must not need a GPU profiler. CONFIRMED.** Counts are decisions, not launches: a CUDA/HIP graph replay runs no Python, so a captured op counts once for the life of the graph. The failure boundary: native fallback is allowed only before the candidate's `run()` begins (discovery failure, version mismatch, decline, selection failure, a kernel that cannot take the contract's call). Once `run()` begins, an exception propagates, because the provider may have partly written the output or launched asynchronous work. #8081 adds that a provider-served exception is labelled with provider, kernel id, digest and config_source, and is neither caught nor retried.
   - Anchors (page): `Observability must not require a GPU profiler merely to determine which implementation served the call.`; `decision_counts() counts decisions, not launches.`; `Once candidate run() begins, an exception propagates to the caller.`
3. **The latency-report rule: all-or-none comparison fields. CONFIRMED, with a precision the steering lacks.** #7654 first added one optional `latency_us` on the candidate, recorded and handed back unchanged, with no influence on routing; a provider with nothing for the call's shape omits it rather than extrapolating; 0, negative, inf and nan are refused at construction; and a test holds that an earlier-listed provider reporting 99999 us beats a later one reporting 1 us. #8081 (six days later) reversed the "no influence" part: `latency_us` may now select **among already-enabled providers**, but only when four more fields accompany it (`baseline_us`, `baseline_path`, `measured_under`, `measurement_ref`), "All four or none". An incomplete report is neither an error nor a rejection: the candidate stays selectable by enable order and just does not compete. A candidate that loses to its own baseline drops out. `check_report(candidate)` returns what a report is missing. The compliance bar written in the RFC comment: a number for this call's shape; finite and positive; structured conditions (graph or eager, cold or warm weights, rounds); a published reproduction command; and the AITER path it was measured against with that path's time under the same conditions. The stated reason: "a number standing alone says a kernel took 7.70us; it does not say whether AITER would have taken 6 or 60", and AITER cannot reconstruct the comparison at dispatch time without running both kernels.
   - Anchors (pr7654): `A provider with nothing for the shape in front of it omits the field rather than extrapolating.`; `0 , a negative, inf and nan are refused at construction.`; `A number a provider picks for itself must not outrank it.` (pr8081): `All four or none — a latency without a baseline says a kernel took 7.70us and leaves open whether AITER would have taken 6 or 60`; `Eligibility stays with people; selection may follow measurement.`; `loses to its own baseline drops out`
4. **Hot-path cost and validation limits, as the author states them. CONFIRMED (as limitations).** The design target of 1 us per provider-served call was not met: 2.42 us for the interface and 2.63 us with five routed ops, measured on a Xeon Platinum 8480C. The CI gate asserts a ratio to one `Signature.bind` in the same process rather than an absolute number, because an absolute 4 us ceiling went red at 4.212 us on a hosted runner on its first run while the dev machine read 2.45 us. The pilot external provider, AKLR, served the routed residual-add contract on MI355X: output matched the op arithmetic to 1.953e-3 with the residual output exact, and its adapter "lives in AKLR's own tree and is not yet upstream there"; every AKLR schema was initially skipped because the device name read "AMD Radeon Graphics". #8081 states: "No provider supplies the comparison fields today", no third-party kernel has been selected and run, and "No deployment with two real providers exists". #8081 is validated on gfx942 (torch 2.9.1); the pilot is on gfx950; nothing in these sources is gfx90a.
   - Anchors (page): `the 1µs target the design proposed is not met , at 2.42µs for the interface`; `The adapter lives in AKLR's own tree and is not yet upstream there.` (pr8081): `No provider supplies the comparison fields today`; `No deployment with two real providers exists.`
5. **Reversal inside the window: the RFC text "latency never influences routing" was superseded by #8081. CONFIRMED.** The same author wrote both; the Nov 17 RFC comment quotes the current text ("It never influences routing") and proposes the reversal (motivation: the MXFP8 tuning tables have zero rows, so a faster provider could not be reached without a framework naming it). Anything citing "no influence on routing" for #7654 alone is correct for 2026-11-11 and stale for 2026-11-17.
   - Anchors (pr7654): `No influence on routing.`; (pr8081): `An incomplete report is not an error and not a rejection`; (pr8081) `With more than one eligible provider, every one is asked.`

## 3. Reported results (as the source states them)

- RFC: hot path 2.42 us per provider-served call (kill switch 0.141 us; `Signature.bind` alone about 4.7 us, per the 0.2 comment), Xeon Platinum 8480C, CPU-only, minimum of repeated runs. Measured on gfx950: allocation inside a graph capture is safe; reading a value back to the host inside capture (`.item()`, `.cpu()`, synchronize) fails and takes the process down, and the failure surfaces far from its cause.
- AKLR pilot: residual-add contract served on MI355X, output to 1.953e-3, residual exact; serving was per shape and mixed (some declined for no measured workload or for speedup below its own serving threshold).
- #8081 validation on gfx942: 188 plugin tests pass (35 new); dispatch overhead 0.155 us with nothing enabled against 0.140 us for the kill switch; two end-to-end tests on real kernels measured AITER's rmsnorm binding at about 94 us against a torch implementation at about 512 us and asserted only the relationship.
- Provider-reported speedups quoted in the RFC comment (19-62% less time on some MXFP8 shapes; Gluon sparse PA 0.55-0.73x of AITER's time) are explicitly "Reported by that library, not verified here", and the author states AITER has not reproduced any of them.
- No end-to-end model number anywhere in these sources.

## 8. Contradiction / adversarial pass

- **One author throughout.** The RFC, all nine retained comments, and both merged PRs are by one contributor, who self-merged #7654 and #8081 and closed the RFC as completed; the review state of the retained text is the author's own self-review sections. The pilot provider's adapter lives in a fork the author commits to ("zufayu/AKLR-1" appears in a commit reference on #7654), which suggests the same person works on both sides (inference from a fork name, not a stated fact).
- **The core claim was reversed within six days** (claim 5): the "no influence on routing" rule in #7654 was removed by #8081. The RFC body at revision 0.3 still says "AITER does not perform quality-based arbitration using Provider-reported evidence", which sits in tension with #8081 selecting by reported latency (the Nov 17 comment frames it as selection among eligible candidates, not arbitration of eligibility); the closing summary lists #8081 as landed.
- **The author's stated shortfalls:** hot-path 2.42 us versus a 1 us target; the first pilot only loaded after a device-name override; the multi-provider path is "measured but under two providers constructed for the test", with no deployment using two real providers.
- **Provider-reported speedups are unverified by AITER** by the author's own words.
- **A catch in the failure rule:** the boundary is the call, not the side effect, because AITER cannot observe whether a side effect occurred; the author calls it conservative, not exact.
- **Reproducibility:** PR #8081's multi-provider tests ask every provider on every call (a behaviour change from first-accept-wins short-circuit when more than one is eligible); cost on the hot path is not re-measured for that case in the retained text.
- No restatement is counted as corroboration. The bearing dive's reading (intake-2828 claim 3) is confirmed by this primary reading and refined: `config_source` and the full four-field baseline set are new details.
