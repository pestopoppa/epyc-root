## 2. Key claims

0. **The planner: tasks are sized by the smallest per-task tile bound T such that the sum over sequences of `min(ceil(t_i / T), max_partitions)` fits the slot capacity. CONFIRMED.** It replaces "one task per sequence plus a tile-proportional share of the remaining slots", under which long sequences keep the longest tasks and a batch ends on one sequence's tail (the author's example: a longest task of 16 tiles against an average of 8.5). The search is a binary search over `[ceil(total/capacity), ceil(total/(capacity - rows))]`, done on the GPU with block reductions. It is on for gfx942 and gfx950 and only when the batch fits one row per planner thread. Equal-length batches keep their task size. Planner refresh cost goes from 3.1-4.6 us to +0.06 to +0.51 us over the old one (the page's own wording: "a planner refresh costs +0.06 to +0.51 us (3.1-4.6 us before)").
   - Anchors (page): `With mixed lengths and about two slots per sequence, long sequences keep the longest tasks.`; `Planner: P2_fp8_d128_p16_H8_B32_ctxU1-8k_tbl32k 1.42x` (diff): `Smallest per-task tile bound T with sum(min(ceil(t / T), P)) <=`; `capacity. ceil(total / capacity) bounds it below;`
1. **One-partition skip: when the plan has `max_partitions == 1`, the tile kernel stores the normalised output directly and the reducer is not launched. CONFIRMED.** Before, such plans wrote packed partials and launched the reducer only to copy them out ("a second dispatch on every call"). It covers FP8 and BF16 on gfx942 and gfx950 (gfx1250 and MXFP4 already did it). Gains are largest where the whole call is only about 6-20 us: gfx942 op-test decode with one partition 19.88 -> 15.83 us (+25.6%) on MI308X; gfx950 64 sequences x 1K FP8 one-partition 1.99x.
   - Anchors (page): `One-partition plans ( max_partitions == 1 ) still wrote packed partials and launched the reducer to copy them out, a second dispatch on every call.`; `MI308X (gfx942, 80 CU) op test decode : FP8 per-tensor, D128, page 16, GQA 8, one partition 19.88 us 15.83 us +25.6%`
2. **Headline: geomean 1.145x over 20 MiniMax-M3 workloads on MI355X, BF16 MTP4 verify up to 1.55x; gfx942 1.024x over 786 cases. CONFIRMED, but the headline aggregates six mechanisms and the planner's share is not isolated. PARTIAL.** The 20-workload table has min 1.016x and BF16 verify rows of +51.9% (b3 q4 32K), +55.3% (b3 q4 225K), +35.7% (b8 q4 32K), most of which come from the gfx950-only fused BF16 verify tile (K32 BF16 MFMA, 2 CTAs per CU) and the gfx950-only MTP slot default, not from the planner. On random-length batches (24 batches of 64-256 sequences, lengths 1K-8K, 1K-32K, 128-2K) the branch is 1.167x (decode 1.103x, verify 1.382x), again with the gfx950 MTP default mixed in. Planner-labelled cases on gfx950: 1.42x (fp8 d128 page-16 H8 B32 random 1K-8K), 1.24x (fp8 d128 page-16 H1 B128 random 1K-8K), 1.18x (fp8 d256 page-64 H8 B32 hinted). On gfx942 the whole branch is 1.024x over 786 cases, one-partition up to 1.49x, random-length MTP4 batches up to 1.27x, worst case 0.969x.
   - Anchors (page): `geomean 1.145x over 20 workloads, BF16 MTP4 verify up to 1.55x`; `MI355X (gfx950, 256 CU) MiniMax-M3 dense-verify-b3-q4-ctx225280, bf16 KV 89.38 us 57.53 us +55.3%`; `random-length MTP4 batches up to 1.27x`; `1.167x (decode 1.103x, verify 1.382x)`
3. **Which parts depend on FP8, MX or gfx950-only hardware. CONFIRMED (read from the text).** Dependent (FP8/MX or CDNA3+/gfx950 ISA): the fused BF16 MTP4 tile (`BF16_MTP_FUSED`: packs K16 pairs into gfx950's K32 BF16 MFMA), `FP8_MTP4_LEAN` / `FP8_MTP4_Q_LDS` / `FP8_MTP4_PAGES_AHEAD` (FP8 verify), the FP8 pack, all MXFP4 paths, nontemporal K/V (measured 0.973x on gfx942, so not even a CDNA3 win), the MTP slot default (gfx942: +25-32% at one sequence but -22% at 16 sequences and 32K tokens), and kernarg preload into user SGPRs. Not dependent: the min-max planner and the one-partition direct store (dtype-agnostic scheduling and epilogue changes, shipped on gfx942), the packed Q absmax (a 16-bit integer max trick, shipped on gfx942 up to head_dim 256), and the host-side scale cache. Two tile groups per CTA need an eight-wave CTA, which the author limits to head_dim 128 on gfx942 because wider heads take up to 512 VGPRs per wave.
   - Anchors (page): `BF16_MTP_FUSED (BF16, D128, page 128, 2-4 M-tiles, no sliding window, positive softmax scale)`; `four of these paths also win and now cover it: one-partition output, the min-max planner, two tile groups (head_dim 128 only) and the packed Q absmax`; `The other changes stay gfx950-only.`
4. **Documented regressions and unexplained gaps. CONFIRMED.** BF16 decode B64 1K-8K is 0.961x and B128 128-2K 0.979x, and the author states the B128 128-2K gap "is not explained by it and was not root-caused". MXFP4 two-group tiles are slower on two short-task shapes (0.964x, 0.967x). The reviewer found FP8 MTP verify with per-tensor scales 0.982x (8 sequences, 32K) and a hot-cache BF16 decode 0.962x. All gfx942 measurements are on MI308X (80 CU), not MI210, ROCm 7.2.0.
   - Anchors (page): `Slower than main: BF16 decode B64 1K-8K 0.961x`; `The B128 128-2K gap is not explained by it and was not root-caused.`; `MI308X (gfx942, 80 CU), ROCm 7.2.0, FlyDSL 0.5.0`
5. **Transfer to our gfx90a verify attention. PARTIAL (derived from our kernel source, not measured).** See sections 4 and 5. In short: the min-max formulation transfers as a sizing rule for the gv split count but only helps under one of two possible KV layouts that a single instrumented call decides; the one-partition skip already exists in our FA vec path and is nearly moot in gv; every ISA-dependent piece has no gfx90a counterpart.

## 8. Contradiction / adversarial pass

- **Self-measured on vendor hardware**, though with a rigorous paired method and a same-tree noise control of +-0.3%. - **Planner caveat:** it applies only to batches that fit one row per planner thread; larger batches keep the proportional split.
- No restatements counted as corroboration. The intake-2828 dive's claim ("principles read, MI355X numbers do not transfer") is confirmed and narrowed: two principles transfer in form (planner, one-partition) but the second is already present in our FA vec path.
