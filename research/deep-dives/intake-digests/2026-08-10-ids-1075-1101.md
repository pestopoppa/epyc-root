## intake-1084#record — RL Forgets! Towards Continual Policy Optimization

- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04364
- **Verification**: dive-overturned
- **Verdict**: worth_investigating (novelty high, relevance medium; categories: training_distillation, benchmark_methodology, multimodal)
- **Disposition**: knowledge_only
- **Expanded from**: intake-1074#record

### Claims (as verified)

0. HEADLINE (abstract, verbatim): 'Experiments on MRCL show that standard reinforcement learning still suffers from severe catastrophic forgetting during continual post-training. We trace this failure to an objective mismatch: the KL regularization used in common policy optimization methods is evaluated on current-task data, whereas forgetting is caused by behavioral drift on prior-task distributions.'
1. SCOPE — the polarity is NOT 'RL forgets more than SFT'. Verbatim: 'Standard RL methods generally retain more prior knowledge than SFT, but still undergo clear performance decay over extended task sequences.' The claim is that RL's advantage over FULL SFT is real but insufficient, not reversed.
2. WHY PRIOR WORK CONCLUDED OTHERWISE — two named METHODOLOGICAL blind spots: benchmark AGE ('several benchmarks are old relative to the base models being adapted ... train on datasets released between 2018 and 2023 while using Qwen2.5-VL, a model released in 2025; such datasets may overlap with modern pretraining corpora') and task HOMOGENEITY ('some studies evaluate RL on narrow task distributions'). MRCL is built exclusively from datasets released 2025 or later as a contamination control.
3. THE KL TERM DOES NOT HELP: a GRPO-with-KL arm (beta=0.04) vs GRPO-without-KL on Qwen3-VL-2B gives MFN 49.64 vs 49.36, a 0.28-point difference. Authors conclude current-task KL 'does not effectively mitigate forgetting; instead, it can reduce model plasticity'.
4. THE FIX — CPO: a replay-free masked-L1 penalty on parameters important to prior tasks, with the protected set grown cumulatively by top-p movement (lambda=100, p=10%). Cost is one binary mask plus one previous-checkpoint copy, explicitly contrasted with Fisher/EWC overhead, storing NO prior-task data. Authors' own caveat: the parameter-movement-as-Fisher-proxy relaxation holds only under 'locally stationary SGD' and approximately uniform per-coordinate coefficients.

### Dive corrections

- DIVE 2026-08-10 (research-intake Stage-2, operator-selected). VERDICT: dive-overturned on three recorded points.
- (1) This entry records the 'distance from base policy' reconciler as UNTESTED. It was tested — in the reconciler paper's own appendix. The recorded gap does not exist.
- (2) This entry files a cold-start result as pro-RL counter-evidence. Its cold-start cell is strongly ANTI-RL; the citation's sign is inverted as filed.
- (3) KL CATEGORY ERROR: an ablation that removes an EXPLICIT KL penalty does not rebut a claim about an IMPLICIT KL bias arising from the update rule itself. The two are different objects and the ablation was recorded here as refuting something it does not address.
- What survives, and is the useful product of this dive: a two-factor regime map. Multi-modality of the target predicts how much supervised fine-tuning damages the base policy; initial rollout pass-rate strictly inside (0,1) predicts whether RL can repair it. The equal-reward filter used by one of the disputing papers selects the exactly-zero-advantage regime (group mean = max reward, so every advantage is identically zero), which reconciles the two headline disagreements: 'nothing moved' is true of the CONTRIBUTION and false of the POLICY at that item.
