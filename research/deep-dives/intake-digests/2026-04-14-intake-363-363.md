EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE (run by the owning session, llama.cpp production-consolidated-v9 @ 0db32c06e3e550065b78311a6031ef3dd2c4f27c, CPU-only Qwen3-1.7B-Q8_0, server started and confirmed terminated): llama-server /v1/chat/completions returns the exact OpenAI logprob shape {id, token, bytes, logprob, top_logprobs[]}; top_logprobs has NO CAP (20, 40 and 64 each returned exactly that many); the reported head is PRE-SAMPLER (temperature 0.0 and 1.0 return byte-identical logprobs, so post_sampling_probs=false gives the unmodified distribution and Eq. 3.1 is not silently distorted); with thinking disabled, 19 of 20 A-T letters appear in the top-20 head with score-token mass 1.0000 and the expectation computes cleanly under the package's own phi (A=20 ... T=1). TWO FINDINGS THAT COST MORE THAN THE CONFIRMATION. (1) On a thinking model the score is NOT at token 0: Qwen3 emitted <think> first, the head contained ZERO score letters, and the expectation was undefined while still returning a well-formed logprob array. This fails silently and must be handled by enable_thinking=false or a forced prefix to the scoring position - see intake-1162#record Appendix C.1, which specifies exactly this prefill for local models. (2) Plumbing was never the blocker; VERIFIER COMPETENCE is. Over 8 correct/buggy code pairs the 1.7B model ranked correct above buggy in 7 of 8, but the gaps were violently bimodal (+0.08, +0.31, +0.96 on three pairs; +15 to +19 on four) with one CONFIDENT INVERSION - correct max(a) scored 10.6 while buggy a[0] scored 18.9. That is n=8, one small model, toy tasks, K=1, C=1: a SMOKE TEST of the mechanism, NOT a measurement, and it must not gate anything. It does relocate the open question from "can llama.cpp serve this" (decisively yes) to "is any local model we would run competent enough to be the verifier", which is the same question intake-875#record answers with a capability threshold. NOT overturned: the reward formula, the three scaling axes, and the direction of the reported gains.
