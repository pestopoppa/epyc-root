# What Does Skill Lift Actually Measure? A Source-Code-Level Audit and Noise-Aware Reanalysis of SkillsBench

7554 paired trials and 1421 configuration–task cells in frozen snapshots. Two controlled grader-dependence examples plus one definition gap averaged 74.6 pp versus 18.7 pp all; the paper did not rerun agents with patched prompts to estimate attributable causal lift. 58% of cells could not register loss. Table V baseline cutoff is +2.92 pp replay, +4.5 shuffle and +0.12 held-out (95% CI −0.67,+0.93); the best-of-two rule showed a significant negative held-out gain. Holdout repeats reuse trials; task bootstraps do not include refitting uncertainty and one-trial pairs have low precision. Label permutations require within-cell exchangeability.

[Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33943v1)

## Evidence checked

- The audit reports placeholder skill directories in four tasks and says one execution harness added 48 built-in skills in most runs. (Abstract, details in §§III-C–III-D).
- No task-level reduction reached statistical significance in the pooled reanalysis after multiple-testing correction. (Abstract, tests in §IV-B).
- For the skill-free-score gating strategy, the audit reports 4–7 percentage points of apparent gain after condition labels were shuffled and no statistically significant improvement on held-out runs. (Abstract, exact strategy values in §V-C Table V).

## Counterevidence and limits

Targeted search found SkillsBench 1.1 publisher release https://www.skillsbench.ai/blogs/skillsbench-1-1 as relevant scope contrast, not an independent reproduction. Audit §VII says its frozen exports differ from the final subset. Treat audited counts and no-detected-negative results as snapshot-specific and distinguish self-generated skills from curated-skill gating.
