# Improving Tail-Augmented Speculative Drafting with Hidden State Mixing and Gradient Detach

HSM mixes target-aligned recurrent and backbone states. HSGD helps early positions while HSM helps later ones in reported shared-parameter joint pretraining + SFT; Figure 1a AL 5.19→5.57 at K=11 and temperature 0. Best frozen-backbone result K=7. Figure 12 four- and eight-step TTT vs HSM = 70.3→36.3, 197.2→43.9 GiB per GPU, measured four GB300s. Memory refers to reserved training memory, not inference.

[Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34092v1)

## Evidence checked

- Hidden State Gradient Detach stochastically blocks gradients through hidden-state and embedding pathways while preserving the forward computation. (Abstract and §5).
- Across four Nemotron models and DeepSeek-V4-Pro, the authors report up to a 52.2% relative gain in mean acceptance length for frozen-backbone HSM-based MTP boosting over co-trained MTP. (Abstract; Fig 1b).
- In the four-step EAGLE-3 Qwen3-8B training comparison on GB300, the authors report peak reserved memory of 70.3 GiB per GPU for TTT and 36.3 GiB for HSM. (§6.6, Figure 12(f)).

## Counterevidence and limits

EAGLE-3 TTT is a strong independent baseline and accepted-length parity is recipe-bound. A 48% per-device relative reduction during a four-device test is insufficient evidence of a feasible single-card training run.

## Derived action for review

Prioritize released boosted checkpoint with compatible format and same serving graph; run early-position and tail acceptance plus latency per chosen K on both devices. If training is required, perform dry-fit accounting and one-step peak reserve check against an explicit margin.
