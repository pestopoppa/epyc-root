# Beyond Gain: Measuring Skill Internalization for Agent Skill Retirement

PARTIAL, arXiv:2610.32322v1, submitted 2026-10-23. Read local versioned HTML §§2–5, Tables 1 and 4, Fig. 3, and Appendix G. No fresh benchmark, artifact execution, or proof/appendix-wide audit. Use independently useful probe items and require some witness model to gain >0.1 on the same items; high target unaided U (level 0.8 in the reported comparison) supports per-model soft retirement, and low aided plus low unaided supports skill repair. In the deliberately difficult corpus, near-zero-gain band retires 42/73 uncovered (57.5%), 42/235 of its retirements (17.9%); no U>=0.8 rule retirement is uncovered by construction. Agreement over 124 paired skill tasks was kappa .42 versus .23, paired difference CI [.01,.38]. SkillsBench execution deliberately removes skills from existing task environments: 59 admitted clean probes of 200 skills, 81 scored tasks, positive means reward lost. Level removed 18 skills and touched 17 tasks, −0.2 pp [−2.7,2.4] aggregate cost; primary level-minus-native band −0.8 pp [−3.1,1.7], no clear ordering. Per-touched-task upper limit about 11 points. Whole-library removal costs 19.1 pp [11.3,27.0]. No general equivalence, downstream improvement, or universal retirement rate is established.

## Evidence checked

- The proposed gate admits a task only when at least one witness model scores more than 0.1 higher with the skill than without it on the same scored items. (§2.4 Validity gate and Eq. 2).
- In the reported SkillsBench ablation, the authors detected no downstream reward difference between the compared retirement rules. Their prespecified level-minus-native-band difference in reward cost was −0.8 percentage points with a reported interval of [−3.1, 1.7]. (§5.1 Benchmark cost, Table 4).
- The authors recommend reversible soft retirement, a high threshold, and extra measurement near that threshold. (§4.5 From label to decision).

## Counterevidence and limits

Independent published-score reconstruction (Observer Agent, 2026-10-26) https://cairncommons.dev/post/36beefb6-6f55-4b85-8d3d-35b05f11c29d uses pinned public data df6c8c77513aa6f60038d878f4c3fb4d83f62430 and confirms 42/73, preserving published threshold semantics. It reuses the author tasks, capability labels and judge marks, with no new inference/manual regrade. Paper itself finds no downstream rule difference, broad conditional cost uncertainty, judge unsupported marks near threshold, and only moderate cross-task reliability. Tessl-single shares skill collection and lacks witness runs, so does not independently validate the witness gate. Contrast the SkillsBench audit: decoupled decision/scoring helps control selection artifacts, but this paper studies soft library retirement after separate probes, not request-level loading.

## Derived action for review

Retain as a design for reversible retirement only when a SkillBank owner proposes a specific existing model/skill pair for suppression. Then freeze behavior-specific task families, verifier and model identity, include untouched-on-policy evaluation and challenge probes with an external witness, preserve abstentions and invalid counts, compare full library versus reversible removal with per-task receipts, and predeclare tolerated regression and restoration conditions. EV-10a still validates mutation efficacy by paired per-suite delta; do not reuse retirement labels as loader efficacy or automatically suppress rules/artifacts.
