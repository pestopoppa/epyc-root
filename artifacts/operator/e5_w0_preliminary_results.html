02

Context capacity — per request, and the maximum each instance can hold

How context is allocated

-c and slots are role-scoped, read once and applied identically to every instance of that role; only thread count varies per instance. So context per request = -c ÷ slots, and it is the same on every shape. What the shape changes is the total: N instances each hold their own -c, so a quarter fleet serves 4× the aggregate context at 4× the KV memory.

RAM is not the constraint — the architecture is

Every deployed model can hold its full 262 144-token trained window on a single quarter instance. * marks a value capped by the model's trained context rather than by memory. Production runs -c 32768 — 12.5% of what the hardware allows — and architect_general halves that again to 8 192 per request via slots: 2.

GLM-5.2 is shown full-machine only: at 222 GiB of weights per private copy it is the one model where a split shape is not a real option, so half and quarter are marked n/a rather than computed.

Note architect_general is the one role storing V in f16, which is why its 62.8 KiB/token is 2.3× the 80B's despite similar geometry. Moving V to q8_0 would roughly halve its KV cost.

The winning shape is model-dependent — do not generalise this

gemma and the 35B favour one full machine at every rung. T=2: 109.80 vs 74.23, and 70.97 vs 50.60. T=4: 168.24 vs 130.71 vs 92.93, and 95.92 vs 85.91 vs 57.27. T=8: 256.77 vs 200.12 vs 150.57, and 124.56 vs 104.53 vs 89.25. The margin does not close as concurrency rises — it widens.

The 80B reverses it. At T=4 two halves give 60.88 against the full machine's 58.04; at T=8, 86.17 against 72.53. The 122B is a tie at T=4 (42.59 vs 42.96). So there is no single fleet-wide shape: the SSM/MoE hybrid behaves differently from the dense-attention MoEs. What holds universally is that quarters lose for every model at every rung measured.

04

Slot behaviour and routing

  • -np is a ceiling, not a fixed cost. With 4 slots configured and 4 active, long-running streams decoded at 43.5 tok/s; the moment two short requests finished, the survivors climbed to ~47 — matching a 2-stream control (46.2 → 48.4). Decode rate tracks slots active at that instant. But -c is divided across slots and KV for all slots is allocated upfront, so width is never free in memory or per-request context.
  • Route sticky per conversation, not to a single instance. The memory and fragmentation constraints apply to load/unload, not to routing among resident instances. What governs routing is prefix-cache locality: llama-server reuses a slot's KV via LCP similarity (453 such selections observed today against 1296 LRU). A follow-up turn sent to a different hot instance discards that prefix and re-prefills from scratch.
  • What that costs. Measured prefill: gemma 8 754 tok at 423.7 tok/s (20.7 s); the 80B at 8 749 tok, 188.7 tok/s (46.4 s); the 80B at 28 232 tok, 107.5 tok/s — 262.7 s. A cache hit re-prefills ~1 token in 76 ms. Spreading distinct conversations across instances is fine; splitting one conversation across them is not.
  • Batching starves long prompts. On gemma at 8k prompts, 25% of streams at np=4 and 29% at np=8 decoded below a quarter of the cell median (as low as 0.32 tok/s) — prefill/decode interference. At short prompts the same widths showed a 1× spread and zero starvation. Slot width is safe to raise for short-prompt roles and hazardous for long-context ones.

A fleet needs --no-mmap to be node-local at all

llama.cpp mmaps the GGUF, so pages are placed once by whichever instance faults first and every later instance inherits that placement regardless of its own --membind. Under shared mmap, fleet throughput therefore depends on instance start order and is nondeterministic across reboots. --no-mmap alone is not enough — without per-instance --membind the private pages just follow default first-touch.

06

Required configuration

  • Placement. frontdoor and ingest_long_context onto taskset -c 0-95 + numactl --interleave=all. Recovers 2.16× and 1.85×, no RAM cost.
  • Weights. Any role running multiple instances against one GGUF needs no_mmap: True and per-instance --membind. worker_general already has this and measures 1.00 locality on all five instances — it is the working reference.
  • Threads. A half-machine instance on -t 96 is SMT-oversubscribed across 48 physical cores; -t 48 measures +13% per-stream and +8.5% aggregate at np=4.
  • Context. Every deployed model can hold 262k on any shape (§02). The present 32k is a policy choice, not a hardware limit — and architect_general's effective 8 192 per request is the tightest window in the fleet, on the role most likely to need a long one.
  • Shape. Model-dependent (§03). Quarters lose everywhere measured; the 80B prefers halves; gemma and the 35B prefer one full machine.
