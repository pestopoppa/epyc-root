# HS-5b — editor-side trainability, downstream of selection

This is a source-based readiness score, not a model-quality measurement or a harness-selection score. Editor-side trainability means training an editor that operates through a particular frozen harness and tool contract. It is independent of HS-5's question, whether that harness ships a first-party weight-space adapter: an external editor can in principle be trained against any candidate. Neither the absence nor presence of an adapter establishes that such training will work.

| Hermes, ACP speakers, oh-my-pi, deepseek-harness | **Potential external editor; frozen training configurations and efficacy unestablished here.** | Their first-party adapter findings and cooperation-surface audits do not answer the separate external-editor question. They are not selected HS-4 shells. |

**Ordering:** select the harness, freeze its source release and the realized contract (including tool schemas and configuration), then train and evaluate the editor against that contract. A change to the harness or schema after training invalidates the original transfer assumption and requires a fresh evaluation; it does not by itself prove that every trained model loses all value. The HS-5b handoff records the prior reported sensitivity and binds the freeze-before-training rule (`intake-1323#record`, `intake-1339#record`). Those prior results are context, not a local result for these candidates.

The existing HS-5 adapter comparison and its historical hardware assessment remain a separate question. The source history's old recommendation to decline rather than bridge missing infrastructure does not override the current operating rule that missing software and platforms are build costs, not decline reasons. A future training proposal must specify and assess the required control plane, training implementation, hardware, fixed contract, baseline and evaluation; it cannot infer feasibility from the existence of an adapter. No training or inference was performed for this score.
