In particular the prior r31 pool3 armed manifest still exists, even though no matching process or grant appeared in a point-in-time exact check. The same source-pinned `legacy_lock.py` (SHA-256 `a6373f02e404c0d92c6ed3033840b41c3c86d841d2c6bd6e789cbde8a326b1f9` in retained r22/r26/r30/r31/r32/r35 copies) creates missing directories and locks with no-follow and inode checks and acquires exclusive flock. The original phasec and relocated owners use the canonical `campaign.serial.lock`. However its descriptor is close-on-exec: the lock becoming available does not prove an old owner’s exec children are drained, and a stale launcher can reenter. A coordinated cutover must establish all legacy descendants have drained, prevent reentry, and retain the canonical lock until all new children and servers drain. The existing resume guard refuses absent locks; the reference routine is evidence, not a completed cutover. This review creates no shared paths or locks. Missing result/unknown scopes remain protected, and no start may be inferred from directory absence alone.
